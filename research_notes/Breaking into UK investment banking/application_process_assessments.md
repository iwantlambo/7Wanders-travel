## 1. Online application stage: what banks ask, cover letters, word limits, rolling admissions

### Takeaway
London applications are a CV plus either a cover letter (about 300-500 words) or 1-5 short motivational questions (usually 150-300 words each). Some banks ask for both, and some ask for a separate application per division. Applications are mostly reviewed on a **rolling basis**: firms such as Nomura say they can close programmes before the deadline once places are filled, so applying in the first weeks after opening (late August to October) matters a lot.

**Continental Europe differences (brief)**
- Spring weeks do not exist in the US, Canada or Latin America. Continental European students usually travel to London for them — [Mergers & Inquisitions: Spring Weeks](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/)
- Paris, Frankfurt and Milan run mostly on **off-cycle internships** posted a few months before they start. London runs structured summer programmes recruited a year ahead — [Fyntraq](https://fyntraq.io/investment-banking-internships-europe). Off-cycle is also the dominant format in DACH — [AnalystClass: DACH internships](https://analystclass.com/internships/dach)

### Inferences
- With rolling review and stated early closures (Nomura), the practical deadline is often weeks before the published one. A first-year should have a finished CV and reusable "why IB / why this bank / why this division" paragraphs ready by early September.
- Because some banks (Citi) take applications per division and limit how many a candidate can submit, students need a defensible divisional choice (IBD vs Markets vs Services) before they apply, not just "investment banking".
- Short motivational answers are effectively the first screen. At about 0.7% acceptance rates, generic answers are unlikely to survive.

- Found no bank-published data quantifying the advantage of applying early (for example, offer rate by application week).
- The J.P. Morgan 630,000 figure is not confirmed against the original Financial News article.

## 2. Online assessments: which tests London banks use (2025-2026), difficulty and preparation

### Takeaway
Nearly every large London bank tests candidates online before any interview. The main reported formats are J.P. Morgan's game-based battery (Pymetrics-style, about 12 mini-games), Morgan Stanley's numerical/verbal/logical tests plus an SJT (Aon cut-e or SHL, sources disagree), Barclays' SHL cognitive, personality and "mindset" assessments, UBS's reasoning tests plus an SJT ("UBS Culture Match"), HSBC's Cappfinity immersive "day in the life" simulation, and Deutsche Bank's SJT, behavioural questionnaire and timed critical/abstract reasoning test. Watson Glaser, Arctic Shores and Sova are widely sold as prep, but this research could **not** confirm any of them at a named London investment bank.

### Cited Findings
- One guide says almost every large London bank runs an online assessment stage before any interview (attribution approximate) — [IBInterviewQuestions: Spring Weeks UK Guide](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/spring-weeks-uk-investment-banking-guide)
- **J.P. Morgan**: London candidates report "a Pymetrics survey where I had to play a number of games" after the online application — [Glassdoor UK: J.P. Morgan Summer Internship](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/J-P-Morgan-Summer-Internship-Interview-Questions-EI_IE145.0,10_KO11,28.htm). Reported as a series of about 12 mini-games of 2-3 minutes each (roughly 20-25 minutes in total), followed by HireVue — [GraduatesFirst: JP Morgan](https://www.graduatesfirst.com/jp-morgan-aptitude-tests); [CareerTestPrep: JP Morgan OA 2026](https://www.careertestprep.com/blog/jp-morgan-online-assessment-guide). J.P. Morgan's own FAQ (about 2 years old) confirms only recorded-interview technology and "for certain job opportunities… a technical assessment" — [J.P. Morgan How We Hire FAQ](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/careers/how-we-hire/faqs)
- **Morgan Stanley**: numerical and SJT tests "provided by SHL or Cut-e (AON)", with some roles adding verbal, logical or inductive tests — [GraduatesFirst: Morgan Stanley](https://www.graduatesfirst.com/morgan-stanley-job-tests); an SHL numerical test profile — [AssessmentDay: Morgan Stanley Numerical](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/profiles/morgan-stanley-numerical.html). One anecdotal WSO account (possibly dated) describes the Aon cut-e sequence as SJT, then verbal, then numerical, then a logical/inductive "find the element that does not fit the pattern" section — [WSO: MS Spring Week Assessment](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/ms-spring-week-assessment)
- **Barclays**: "Cognitive Ability, Personality assessment, and Mindset assessment tests. Provided by SHL" — [JobTestPrep: Barclays](https://www.jobtestprep.co.uk/barclays-online-tests). Another prep site found no evidence of game-based assessments at Barclays. It notes a Barclays-branded HireVue tenant exists but could not confirm HireVue use for 2026-27 early careers — [GameAssessmentPrep: Barclays](https://www.gameassessmentprep.com/employers/barclays)
- **UBS**: UBS's own pages reportedly describe verbal, numerical and inductive-logical reasoning tests plus an SJT called "UBS Culture Match", delivered via SHL TalentCentral. Claims that UBS uses Arctic Shores or pymetrics games are described as unsourced — [CareerTestPrep: UBS](https://www.careertestprep.com/knowledge/ubs-aptitude-test); [GameAssessmentPrep: UBS](https://www.gameassessmentprep.com/employers/ubs)
- **HSBC**: graduates take a Cappfinity-built immersive simulation of a working day. SHL OPQ32 is used for experienced hires — [TestSolve: HSBC](https://www.testsolve.ai/companies/hsbc/). London spring week candidates report an online assessment, then a HireVue with 2-3 questions, then a telephone interview — [Glassdoor: HSBC Spring Week](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/HSBC-Spring-Week-Interview-Questions-EI_IE3482.0,4_KO5,16.htm)
- **Deutsche Bank**: reported to start with an SJT, then a behavioural questionnaire and a timed ability test on "critical reasoning skills". Only the ability test is timed, and it includes a "challenging abstract reasoning section". DB does not name the vendor — [GameAssessmentPrep: Deutsche Bank](https://www.gameassessmentprep.com/employers/deutsche-bank); [JobTestPrep: Deutsche Bank SJT](https://www.jobtestprep.co.uk/deutsche-bank)
- **Citi**: sources conflict. JobTestPrep describes a "Plum Assessment" — [JobTestPrep: Citi](https://www.jobtestprep.co.uk/citi-assessment-centre). Another prep source cites Korn Ferry (Talent-Q) numerical and logical reasoning. A UK guide says numerical and verbal reasoning, sometimes with an SJT or personality test — [Finbound: Citi Summer Internship](https://www.finbound.org/blog/citi-summer-internship-application-guide)
- **Bank of America**: prep sites claim SHL numerical tests, but one notes "no official page, vendor case study or candidate invitation confirms this" — [GameAssessmentPrep: Bank of America](https://www.gameassessmentprep.com/employers/bank-of-america). A London candidate reported an online assessment that was mostly behavioural questions with some numerical problems — [Glassdoor: BofA Summer Analyst](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Bank-of-America-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE8874.0,15_KO16,30.htm)
- **Watson Glaser**: 40 questions in 30 minutes, described as the most widely used critical-reasoning test and common in law and banking (no named London IB confirmed) — [AssessmentDay: Watson Glaser](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/watson-glaser-critical-thinking.htm)
- **Cappfinity** is most common in the legal sector and at Deloitte/EY — [AssessmentDay: Capp](https://www.assessmentday.co.uk/capp.htm). HSBC is the main banking example found (above).
- **Arctic Shores** (game-based): cited users are Deloitte, PwC (legacy) and Capgemini, with no bank named — [JobTestPrep: Arctic Shores](https://www.jobtestprep.co.uk/arctic-shores-assessment). **Sova** is a UK provider not tied to any specific bank in sources found — [CareerTestPrep: Sova](https://www.careertestprep.com/knowledge/sova-assessment)
- Banking prep providers cover SHL, Aon (cut-e), Korn Ferry (Talent-Q), Cappfinity and Pymetrics, which suggests these are the main publishers banks use (vendor claim) — [JobTestPrep: Banking Summer Internship Tests](https://www.jobtestprep.co.uk/banking-summer-internship)
- Pass rates: one low-reliability guide says 50-60% of UBS candidates who reach the video stage advance to an assessment centre — [GetSmartResume: UBS](https://www.getsmartresume.com/article/ubs-graduate-talent-program). No bank publishes test pass rates.

### Inferences
- Classic aptitude tests (numerical, verbal, inductive, SJT) are still the main format at most London banks. Game-based testing is mainly a J.P. Morgan feature among the bulge brackets, and possibly Citi (if "Plum" is correct).
- Preparation should be vendor-specific. Read the invitation email to find the provider (SHL, Aon/cut-e, Cappfinity, Pymetrics/Harver), then practise that publisher's question styles under timed conditions. One 2026 spring-week guide in the search results suggests about 10-15 hours of format-specific practice (provenance not verified).
- SJTs and the J.P. Morgan games appear to score fit with the bank's stated values. Reading each bank's published values or "business principles" before the test is low-cost and directly relevant.
- Game-based tests (J.P. Morgan) are hard to "study" for. The useful preparation is familiarity with the game types and reading instructions carefully. The numerical tests reward speed with percentages, ratios and table/chart reading.

### Gaps
- No official 2025/26 vendor confirmation for any bulge-bracket London bank. All vendor attributions come from prep sites or candidate posts.
- Pymetrics is understood to have been acquired by Harver, and J.P. Morgan's games may now be branded Harver. This was not verified in this session.
- No reliable pass rates or cut-off scores for any bank's online tests were found.
- Citi's current provider (Plum vs Talent-Q vs SHL) is unresolved.

## 3. Video interviews (HireVue and similar): format, questions, AI scoring, policy changes

### Takeaway
One-way recorded video interviews are standard at the second stage. They usually have 3-7 questions, about 30 seconds of preparation and 2-3 minutes per answer, and often allow only one attempt. Questions are mostly motivational and competency-based, with an occasional "market you follow" prompt. HireVue stopped facial-expression analysis in 2021, and any automated scoring is now said to be transcript and language-based, with humans reviewing shortlists. Banks do not publish whether their HireVues are machine-scored. Goldman explicitly bans external aids such as ChatGPT or Google during interviews.

### Cited Findings
- **Goldman Sachs**: HireVue pre-assessment gives 30 seconds of prep and a 2-minute response time, per eFinancialCareers research — [Yahoo/Fortune: Goldman wants students to stop using ChatGPT](https://www.yahoo.com/news/goldman-sachs-wants-students-stop-210747496.html). A 2026 spring-week guide reports 5-6 questions (30 seconds prep, up to 2 minutes each): an introduction, "Why Goldman Sachs and why this division", 2-3 competency questions (teamwork, conflict, problem-solving, overcoming challenges) and one commercial-awareness question about something that recently caught your interest in markets. It says the questions are not technical — [OurGen: Goldman Sachs Spring Week 2026](https://ourgen.uk/insights/goldman-sachs-spring-week)
- **Bank of America**: reported format is 30 seconds prep and up to 3 minutes per answer, with 3-5 questions — [GameAssessmentPrep: BofA](https://www.gameassessmentprep.com/employers/bank-of-america). An August 2026 Glassdoor entry describes a first-round HireVue of about 15 minutes with four scenarios — [Glassdoor: BofA Summer Analyst](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Bank-of-America-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE8874.0,15_KO16,30.htm). London spring week HireVue: about 5 timed questions — [Glassdoor: BofA Spring Week](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Bank-of-America-Spring-Week-Interview-Questions-EI_IE8874.0,15_KO16,27.htm)
- **J.P. Morgan**: guides report 3-5 questions with one retry per question (anecdotal), and not every applicant gets the same questions — [Leland: JP Morgan HireVue](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/jp-morgan-hirevue); [IGotAnOffer: JPM HireVue](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/jpm-hirevue-interview). In London spring week reports, offers were sent directly after the HireVue, with no further interview — [Glassdoor: J.P. Morgan Spring Week](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/J-P-Morgan-Spring-Week-Interview-Questions-EI_IE145.0,10_KO11,22.htm). - **Morgan Stanley** spring insight (London): online test, then HireVue with 5-7 questions, then a Zoom interview. Another account reports no live interview — [Glassdoor: Morgan Stanley Spring Insight](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Morgan-Stanley-Spring-Insight-Interview-Questions-EI_IE2282.0,14_KO15,29.htm)
- **Deutsche Bank** (UK and APAC): 2 minutes to prepare and 2 minutes to record per question, one attempt. Elsewhere it may be a live virtual meeting instead — [GameAssessmentPrep: Deutsche Bank](https://www.gameassessmentprep.com/employers/deutsche-bank)
- **UBS**: pre-recorded video interview of 4-6 questions — [CareerTestPrep: UBS](https://www.careertestprep.com/knowledge/ubs-aptitude-test). Freshminds says UBS's HireVue-based AI assessment is "controversial" but still in use as of 2025 — [Freshminds event review](https://www.freshminds.co.uk/blog/2026/05/event-review-investment-banking-and-the-application-arms-race)
- **HireVue AI scoring**: HireVue stopped scoring facial expressions, announcing it in January 2021 after an FTC complaint and an independent audit — [Revoxy case study](https://revoxy.ai/case-studies/case-hirevue-facial-analysis/); [The AI Table](https://www.theaitable.org/blog/ai-facial-analysis-hiring). Secondary sources say scoring now centres on the transcript, assessed against an employer-configured competency model for relevance, structure and specificity. The ranked shortlist is then reviewed by humans — [Capstone Workforce: How HireVue scoring works (2026)](https://capstoneworkforce.com/job-seekers/hirevue-interview-scoring)
- Whether Goldman's HireVues are reviewed by AI or humans is disputed on forums, and Goldman has not confirmed either way — [PrepLounge forum](https://www.preplounge.com/finance-forum/is-goldman-sachs-hirevue-actually-reviewed-by-humans-or-ai-22448)
- A HireVue executive (Bloomberg, November 2025) questioned why candidates cannot use AI in the pre-hire application — [Detroit News (Bloomberg syndication)](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/2025/11/20/wall-street-wants-everyone-using-ai-except-its-job-applicants/87370348007/)
- Non-UK comparison: South Korean banks (for example Shinhan) dropped personal-statement questions for AI-assessed video interviews, with failing applicants reviewed by humans (September 2026) — [FN News (Korea)](https://en.fnnews.com/news/202609131402172034)

### Inferences
- Treat a HireVue as a structured interview scored on content. Answer the actual question within about 30 seconds and use a tight STAR structure in about 90-120 seconds. Name the bank, the division and specifics, because transcript-based scoring and human reviewers both reward relevance and concrete detail.
- Prepare a short "market story": one company, deal or macro trend explained in under 2 minutes. Goldman, J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley spring HireVues all report a commercial-awareness prompt.
- One attempt per question (DB, often others) means rehearsing on camera beforehand is the most useful single preparation.

### Gaps
- No bank publicly states whether its HireVue responses are machine-scored, human-scored or both.
- No official HireVue time limits from any bank. Figures are from candidate and prep-site reports.
- Could not confirm the reported 24-48-hour completion windows for HireVue invitations.

## 4. Assessment centres and superdays (and the spring-week end-of-week assessment)

### Takeaway
London final rounds are a half-day or full-day assessment centre or "superday". Typically there are 2-4 back-to-back interviews of 20-45 minutes. Boutiques such as Rothschild and Lazard add a group case exercise (4-8 candidates) and/or an individual case presentation with about 60 minutes of preparation. Some banks (UBS) add a timed M&A case study. Final rounds are moving back **in person**, and case-study turnaround has shrunk from days to hours because of AI. On spring weeks, conversion is usually via an end-of-week interview or assessment that grants either a direct internship offer or a **fast track** that skips the online tests and video stages. A fast track is not an offer.

### Cited Findings
**Format**
- In London the final stage is typically a full-day assessment centre rather than a US-style superday. It includes a group exercise of 4-6 candidates on a business case, observed for collaboration as much as analytical output (attribution approximate) — [IBInterviewQuestions: IB in London](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/investment-banking-london-uk-guide); [SuperdayReady: IB recruiting by region](https://www.superdayready.com/blog/investment-banking-recruiting-by-region)
- **Rothschild & Co** UK/Europe AC: half-day, in person, with a group case study (assessing collaboration, not dominance), an individual case presentation with 60 minutes of prep (investment recommendation), and 2-3 interviews with Directors and MDs (attribution approximate) — [Extern: Rothschild internship guide](https://www.extern.com/post/rothschild-and-co-internship-guide); [Finbound: Rothschild](https://www.finbound.org/blog/rothschild-summer-internship-application-guide). Strong spring performers are offered a fast-track assessment for the next summer's internship (same sources).
- **Lazard** (official): interns give a 10-minute presentation to a panel of senior bankers in the final week, which together with internship performance determines the offer. London spring insight lasts 3 days and is for first-years (or second-years on 4-year courses) — [Lazard UK early careers](https://www.lazard.com/about-lazard/locations/united-kingdom/careers-in-the-united-kingdom/early-career-programmes-in-the-uk/). *Historical (older forum):* the London AC had a 4-8-person group exercise on a roughly 15-page pack, followed by an individual presentation — [WSO: Lazard London AC](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forums/lazard-london-assessment-centre)
- **Goldman Sachs** London: a superday of 3 x 20-minute interviews with different teams in the office — [Glassdoor: Goldman Sachs London](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Goldman-Sachs-London-Interview-Questions-EI_IE2800.0,13_IL.14,20_IM1035.htm). IB superdays are described as back-to-back interviews with bankers of mixed seniority and as "mostly behavioral" — [Exponent: GS IB Summer Analyst guide](https://www.tryexponent.com/guides/goldman-sachs-investment-banking-summer-analyst-interview)
- **Barclays** IB summer analyst final round: 2 interviews of 20-30 minutes each — [Glassdoor UK: Barclays IB Summer Analyst](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/Barclays-Investment-Banking-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE3456.0,8_KO9,42.htm)
- **J.P. Morgan**: an AC of 3-4 rounds with the division. A 2025 London markets intern reported 3 technical interviews, each in the interviewer's asset class. The process took about 4 weeks — [Glassdoor UK: J.P. Morgan Summer Intern](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/J-P-Morgan-Summer-Intern-Interview-Questions-EI_IE145.0,10_KO11,24.htm)
- **UBS** London: a timed M&A case study and often a group exercise — [Aced: UBS IB Summer Analyst guide](https://www.aced.io/guides/ubs-investment-banking-summer-analyst-interview)
- **Evercore** London spring insight: 3 days, with skills workshops, a mock M&A transaction, and CV and interview workshops — [Evercore Students & Graduates Europe/Asia](https://www.evercore.com/careers/students-graduates/students-graduates-europe-asia/)
- **In-person shift**: superdays are "returning to the traditional in-person format". **Spring week conversion / fast track**
- Banks do not publish spring week conversion rates. Fast-track status typically bypasses online tests and the video interview and goes straight to a final interview or AC. It is not an offer and depends on performance during the week (attribution approximate) — [Intervyo: Spring Week](https://www.intervyo.co.uk/programmes/spring-week); [IBInterviewQuestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/spring-weeks-uk-investment-banking-guide)
- Mergers & Inquisitions: roughly half of participants advance to an AC and about half of those win an internship, but "the average range is probably more like 20-40%" — [M&I: Spring Weeks](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/)
- One estimate table (low reliability): Citi about 80-90%, Barclays about 60%, BofA about 45%, Goldman about 20%. A separate source puts BofA IB nearer 30% and Barclays, Jefferies and Lazard as high. A 2025 Trackr cohort reportedly showed 31% converting directly plus 38% fast-tracked (attribution approximate) — [GetJobPing: Spring vs Summer](https://www.getjobping.com/blog/spring-week-vs-summer); [WSO: Most convertible spring weeks](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/most-convertible-spring-weeks)

- Spring weeks are helpful but not required. It is possible to win London internship offers without one — [M&I: Spring Weeks](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/)

### Inferences
- For group exercises, assessors mark listening, building on others' points, time-keeping and reaching a defensible group answer. Dominating is penalised. A useful habit is to summarise the group's position and keep track of time aloud.
- Individual case presentations (Rothschild, Lazard style) reward a clear recommendation up front, 2-3 supporting reasons with numbers from the pack, key risks, and a conclusion that fits the time limit.
- Treat a spring week itself as a multi-day assessment: daily networking, the end-of-week interview and any group task all feed the conversion decision.
- With in-person finals and shorter case windows, candidates must be able to do the analysis unaided, quickly, and with basic Excel/PowerPoint skills.

## 5. Interview content split: motivational, competency, commercial awareness, technical (spring vs summer vs full-time)

### Takeaway
Spring week processes are almost entirely motivational ("why IB / why this bank / why this division") plus competency (STAR), with light commercial awareness ("a company/stock/news story you follow"). Technicals are rare. Summer internship interviews in London keep the same motivational and competency core but add basic technicals: the three valuation methods, walk me through a DCF, how changes flow through a DCF, and simple accounting. Expect more of these in IBD and boutiques (Rothschild, Lazard) and asset-class-specific technicals in Markets. Some London ACs reportedly skip technicals entirely, so prepare for both.

- Rough weighting for a first/second-year, inferred from the reports above: **spring week** about 60% motivational, 30% competency, 10% commercial awareness, near 0% technical. **Summer internship (IBD)** about 35% motivational/CV, 30% competency, 15% commercial (a deal or market view), 20% basic technical. **Full-time/off-cycle** is likely more technical, with the expectation that you can discuss your previous internship's deals in depth. These splits are inferences, not sourced figures.
- Minimum technical toolkit for a summer IBD interview: the three statements and how they link, the three valuation methods and which is typically highest, walk me through a DCF, WACC/terminal value logic, EV vs equity value, and basic M&A accretion/dilution.
- For commercial awareness, have one deal (ideally a UK/European deal the bank advised on), one market view (rates, FX or equities) and one company or stock pitch ready.

## 6. UK CV norms for banking applications

### Takeaway
One page with a simple single-column layout. Education first, with predicted or actual degree classification and A-level (or equivalent) grades and subjects. Then experience (part-time jobs count), leadership/societies, and skills and interests. British spelling, no photo. Screeners spend seconds on a first pass: the Ladders eye-tracking study measured 7.4 seconds in 2018, and banking guides suggest 20-30 seconds. Concrete, quantified bullets and visible grades matter most. AI use has made CVs look "almost identical", so specificity is what differentiates.

### Cited Findings
- A first-year CV longer than one page is "a screen-out at effectively every bank". State degree classification or predicted classification, list A-level grades, use British spelling and day-month-year dates, and do not include a photo. A-levels "carry far more weight at spring week stage than at any later point", and A*A*A in quantitative subjects is a genuine signal. A part-time job, a society committee role and a sport are "perfectly adequate raw material" (attribution approximate between these two guides) — [Calibre CV: Spring Week CV Guide 2026](https://calibrecv.co.uk/blog/spring-week-cv-guide); [Finbound: Spring Week CV Guide](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-cv-guide)
- Guides cite a 30-second read by bankers and a roughly 20-second first-year filter (attribution approximate) — [Calibre CV](https://calibrecv.co.uk/blog/spring-week-cv-guide); [M&I: IB resume template](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/free-investment-banking-resume-template/)
- Finance-society activity, a personal portfolio or trading simulations show genuine engagement, and quantified achievements help — [Finbound: Spring Week CV Guide](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-cv-guide); [Bright Network: IB CV template](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/career-path-guides/investment-banking/investment-banking-cv-template/)
- The Ladders 2018 eye-tracking study found an average initial screen of 7.4 seconds. Simple layouts, clear sections and headings performed best; cluttered layouts, multiple columns and long sentences hurt — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/). A critique argues the figure reflects a rejection filter rather than total reading time — [ERE: Is the 6-second resume scan a myth?](https://www.ere.net/articles/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth)
- AI use in applications has made CVs look "almost identical", making scoring less consistent — [Freshminds event review](https://www.freshminds.co.uk/blog/2026/05/event-review-investment-banking-and-the-application-arms-race)

### Inferences
- A practical first-year structure: Education (university, course, expected classification, relevant modules, first-year results if available; A-levels/IB with grades; GCSE summary) → Experience (any job, with numbers) → Leadership and Activities (society roles, sport, volunteering) → Skills, Languages and Interests (Excel, any coding, languages with level, 2-3 specific interests that can start a conversation).
- Common mistakes: two pages, multi-column or graphic templates, missing grades (readers will assume they are weak), vague duties instead of results, and US formats (GPA, "resume") without UK equivalents.
- Given the second-scale first pass, put grades and the strongest line at the top, and keep each bullet to one line.

### Gaps
- No bank-specific or credible source on whether London banks use keyword-based ATS filtering of CVs, or how. Search results only offered generic "ATS-friendly" advice.
- The Ladders study is US, general-industry and from 2018. No banking-specific screening-time study was found.

## 7. Candidate use of generative AI: banks' stated policies and recruiter responses (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Banks broadly tolerate AI for research and proofreading but prohibit it in live and timed stages. Goldman Sachs is the clearest case: since at least June 2025 it has told campus applicants that external sources, including ChatGPT or Google, are prohibited during interviews, with disqualification possible. Recruiters are responding with in-person superdays, shorter case-study windows, detection software and more reliance on tests and video. About half of UK students now use generative AI in applications, while ISE data shows most employers either allow AI use or have given no guidance.

### Cited Findings
- **Goldman Sachs (June 2025)**: an EMEA campus recruiting email (private investing academy) stated: "Goldman Sachs prohibits the use of any external sources, including ChatGPT or Google search engine, during the interview process." Guidance warned that failure to adhere "may result in disqualification". A spokesperson said the language is "consistent with what we send to any of our campus applicants across all positions" and "We want to hear from our applicants in their own voice." Amazon and Anthropic also warn candidates — [Fortune, 11 June 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/06/11/goldman-sachs-students-ai-chatgpt-interviews-amazon-anthropic); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/goldman-sachs-wants-students-stop-210747496.html)
- **Bloomberg (20 November 2025)**: Goldman shared guidelines with internal recruiters that candidates caught using AI in interviews should be disqualified. Firms are deploying detection software, returning superdays to in-person and cutting case-study windows from days to hours. TestGorilla data: about 15% of 5M+ candidates assessed across industries were flagged for "potentially suspicious activity" including AI help, with finance a few points higher — [Detroit News](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/2025/11/20/wall-street-wants-everyone-using-ai-except-its-job-applicants/87370348007/); [Daily Herald](https://www.dailyherald.com/20251122/business/wall-street-wants-everyone-using-ai-except-its-job-applicants/); [Bloomberg original](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-20/wall-street-wants-everyone-using-ai-except-its-job-applicants)
- **ISE Student Recruitment Survey 2025** (155 employers, 31,000+ hires, 1.8M+ applications): 49% of employers allow candidates to use AI, 10% have banned it or added technical blocks, 45% give no guidance, and 79% are redesigning or reviewing their processes because of AI — [ISE: 5 trends from Recruitment Survey 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/498/5_trends_you_need_to_know_from_ises_recruitment_survey_2025/); [HR Grapevine](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/content/article/ise-2025-10-21-whats-really-happening-in-graduate-recruitment-ais-role)
- **Bright Network 2025** (14,000+ students/graduates): over 50% had used GenAI in job applications, up from 38% in 2024 — [Bright Network (Guardian coverage)](https://employers.brightnetwork.co.uk/news/featured-in-the-guardian-half-of-graduates-using-ai-for-job-applications/). Some employers use AI detectors, especially traditional fields such as Magic Circle law, and flagged applications are "likely to be rejected". Even without detectors, screeners "get to know the patterns of AI-generated content very quickly" — [Bright Network AI guide](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/graduate-career-advice/complete-guide-ai-for-job-applications/)
- **Bank of England** (UK central bank, not an IB) publishes permitted uses: researching the Bank, and proofreading or refining CVs and answers, used "responsibly" — [Bank of England: How to apply](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/careers/how-to-apply)
- **Lloyds Banking Group** runs a Candidate Preparation Hub explaining expectations, noting candidates are "uncertain about the role AI should play" — [Careers & Enterprise Company](https://www.careersandenterprise.co.uk/early-career-recruitment-ai-enabled-workplace-view-next-generation-recruitment-team)
- **Sector response (UK)**: firms are exploring "AI-assisted screening tools to video submissions and gamified assessments" with no consensus yet — [Freshminds event review, May 2026](https://www.freshminds.co.uk/blog/2026/05/event-review-investment-banking-and-the-application-arms-race)
- The eFinancialCareers *Inside Knowledge* 2026/27 guide says AI is both shrinking internship numbers and making mass-applying easier, citing a student who made 500+ applications with AI — [Imperial-hosted eFinancialCareers guide 2026/27](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/careers-service/public/resources/STUDENT-CAREERS-GUIDE-2026-27-prf4.pdf)

### Inferences
- Safe practice for a student: using AI to research banks and deals, to practise mock interviews and to proofread is broadly accepted. Using it to write answers wholesale is risky, because answers look identical and some detection is in use. Using it during any live or timed stage (HireVue, online tests, live interviews, case studies) should be treated as disqualifying, given Goldman's explicit rule and the industry trend.
- Expect written application answers to carry less weight, and tests, video and in-person performance to carry more. Every claim in a written answer should be something the candidate can defend aloud at an in-person final.

### Gaps
- No published candidate-AI policy was found for J.P. Morgan, Morgan Stanley, Barclays, Citi, BofA, UBS, HSBC or Deutsche Bank. Their portals and FAQs could not be fetched.
- No London-specific (Financial News, FT) article on bank AI-detection was retrieved. Coverage found is US-led (Bloomberg/Fortune) or general UK graduate-market data (ISE, Bright Network).
- No data on how many UK banks use AI-text detectors on written answers.
