# Win London's Investment Banking Race Early

For a first- or second-year undergraduate targeting London, the most important fact on 12 October 2026 is that the window that decides most outcomes is open now. Spring-week applications for Easter 2027 are being reviewed on a rolling basis, and J.P. Morgan (1 November), UBS (31 October) and possibly Morgan Stanley (as early as 18 October) close within weeks. A spring week is the cheapest way to lock in a summer 2028 internship roughly 15 months ahead. The odds are steep. UK finance applicants needed about **101 applications per internship offer** in Trackr's 2026 survey, **49% of spring-week applicants received no offer**, and the **top 10% of candidates won 40% of offers**. Offers concentrate among candidates who prepare early and deeply, not among those who apply most. To get hired you must clear a mostly HR-run funnel: a CV with short written answers, vendor-specific online tests, a recorded video interview, then an in-person assessment centre. Spring-week interviews are almost entirely about motivation and competencies. Summer interviews add core technicals: the three statements, enterprise value, DCF, multiples, accretion/dilution and the LBO. Standing out in 2026 is a separate problem. AI has made CVs "almost perfectly the same", banks have moved final rounds back in person and banned AI during live stages, and UBS now formally requires practical AI skills for its 2027 intake. The differentiator that still works is depth you can verify: a model, a stock pitch and a view on a UK deal that you can defend for ten minutes. Add UK-specific technical points that US-centric prep leaves out, such as IFRS 16 leases, IFRS 18's cash-flow changes from 1 January 2027, and £ inputs at the 25% UK tax rate. The 2026 market gives you a ready story. European deal value is near multi-year highs while deal counts fall, foreign and PE buyers are bidding for UK plcs, the London IPO market is thin, and the Bank of England is holding at 3.75% with a growing risk of a hike. Many dates and figures below come from third-party trackers, prep providers or forums rather than from banks. They are flagged as tracker-reported, estimated or contested, and each bank's careers page is the only authoritative source for dates.

## The decisive window is open now and closes within weeks

London recruiting runs about 18 months ahead of the job itself. Easter 2027 spring weeks feed summer 2028 internships, which feed analyst start dates in 2029. Almost every stage is reviewed on a rolling basis, so a stated deadline is a ceiling, not a target.

### Decode the year labels before you apply

Banks label programmes by the year they run, which confuses almost everyone. A **"2027 Spring Week"** is the Easter 2027 programme for students in the **first year of a three-year degree or the second year of a four-year degree**, typically graduating in 2029. Rothschild & Co, Deutsche Bank, UBS, BNP Paribas, Citi and Morgan Stanley all state a version of this rule on their London listings ([ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/spring-weeks-uk-investment-banking-guide)). A **"2027 Summer Analyst"** role is the ten-week internship in summer 2027 for students who are in their penultimate year now. Today's first-years on three-year degrees will apply for **"2028 Summer Analyst"** roles from about August 2027. UBS's 2027 spring programme is for the class of 2029 and explicitly "leads into the 2028 summer internship" ([Gorizzume](https://gorizzume.co.uk/spring-weeks)). The rules have some give. Trackr's 2026 survey found that **29% of successful spring interns were beyond first year** (24% second-years, 5% third-years), so second-years on longer or non-standard courses should check each listing rather than assume they are excluded ([Trackr](https://the-trackr.com/blog/spring-weeks-season-insights-2026)).

### The funnel is narrow, and offers cluster at the top

The global numbers are stark. Goldman Sachs received **360,000+ applications for about 2,600 intern seats in 2025 (0.7%)**, and acceptance stayed below 1% for a third straight year in 2026, with about 2,500 interns drawn from 500+ universities ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/landing-goldman-sachs-internship-difficult-nasa-astronaut-acceptance); [Yahoo Finance/Fortune](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/exclusive-goldman-sachs-intern-acceptance-120000373.html)). J.P. Morgan reportedly received about 630,000 applications for about 4,100 places. That figure is secondary reporting of a Financial News number and was not verified at source ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/goldman-sachs-internship-acceptance-harder-than-harvard/493960)). The UK data is more useful because it shows how candidates actually experience the funnel. In Trackr's 2026 summer survey of 296 UK finance applicants, the average candidate made about **74 applications, reached 4.1 interviews and won about 0.7 offers**, or **101 applications per offer**, the first time the figure has gone above 100 ([Trackr](https://the-trackr.com/blog/summer-internship-season-report-2026/)). In the spring-week survey of 232 candidates, applicants averaged 35 applications for 1.03 offers (down from 1.52 a year earlier), 49% received nothing, and the top decile took 40% of all offers ([Trackr](https://the-trackr.com/blog/spring-weeks-season-insights-2026)). Across all UK sectors, the Institute of Student Employers recorded a record **140 applications per graduate vacancy** in 2025 ([ISE](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/410/record_graduate_job_applications/)). Trackr's samples are small and self-selected, but the pattern is consistent. A few well-prepared candidates collect several offers while most collect none. The volume of applications matters far less than how good each one is and how early it arrives.

### A spring week is an option on summer 2028, not an offer

Spring weeks convert into summer internships in two ways: a direct offer, or a **fast track** that skips the online tests and video interview and goes straight to a final round. Trackr's 2025 cohort showed **31% direct conversion, 38% fast-tracked and 31% rejected** ([Trackr 2025 PDF](https://the-trackr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Spring-Weeks-Season-Insights-2025.pdf)). In 2026 direct conversion fell to **20%**, with a further **44% fast-tracked** ([Trackr](https://the-trackr.com/blog/spring-weeks-season-insights-2026)). Mergers & Inquisitions puts the average at "probably more like 20–40%" ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/)). Firm-level figures circulating in guides (Citi 80–90%, Barclays about 60%, J.P. Morgan about 50%, BofA about 45%, Goldman about 20%) are **unsourced estimates**, and one UK guide stresses that "a fast-track is not an offer" ([Whali](https://whali.co.uk/blog/spring-week-guide)). Lazard and Piper Sandler say on their own pages that spring participants can be fast-tracked into the following summer's process ([Lazard](https://www.lazard.com/about-lazard/locations/united-kingdom/careers-in-the-united-kingdom/early-career-programmes-in-the-uk/); [Piper Sandler](https://www.pipersandler.com/careers/students/early-careers-london)). Conversion further down the line matters too, because banks fill most analyst classes from their intern classes ([ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/ib-summer-internship-applications)). Intern-to-full-time return rates are **contested**. A recruiter blog cites 70–80% at bulge brackets and over 90% at some boutiques ([Elite Careers Strategy](https://elitecareersstrategy.medium.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-enter-investment-banking-from-a-recruiter-who-placed-candidates-at-goldman-1d595ed0dd4e)). A UK calendar says about 50% ([City Investment Training](https://www.cityinvestmenttraining.com/post/investment-banking-recruiting-calendar-uk-europe-2025-26-guide)). Unverified 2025 London forum reports range from 30% at Morgan Stanley to 85–90% at BofA ([WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/london-return-offer-2025)). The falling direct-conversion rate is the signal to note: a spring week is a large head start, not a guarantee.

### Pick banks by tier, because each tier plays a different game

London practitioners group banks into four tiers. The **bulge brackets** are Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and J.P. Morgan at the top, then BofA, Citi, Barclays, Deutsche Bank and UBS. The **elite boutiques** are led in the UK by Rothschild & Co and Lazard, which M&I calls "the top two elite boutiques in the U.K.", plus Evercore, Centerview, PJT, Moelis and Perella Weinberg. The **middle market** includes Jefferies, Houlihan Lokey, Piper Sandler, Baird and William Blair, with deals of roughly $100m–$1bn. Finally there are European "in-between" banks such as BNP Paribas and Société Générale, and UK brokers such as Peel Hunt, Investec and TD Securities ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-in-london/); [PrepLounge](https://www.preplounge.com/en/blog/finance/investment-banking/firms/investmentbanks-uk); [ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/bulge-bracket-vs-boutique-vs-middle-market)). These tiers are practitioner opinion, not league-table fact, but the strategic point holds. Bulge brackets run the largest spring and summer classes across several divisions, often with one application per firm. Boutiques run short programmes (Evercore's and Lazard's spring programmes are three days each) with few seats, a heavy M&A focus and high conversion. Mid-market banks and brokers often close later, which makes them a sensible hedge rather than an afterthought.

### Placements, off-cycle roles and continental Europe are real second doors

Students on sandwich degrees should know that **12-month industrial placements** recruit as early as spring weeks. UBS's 2027 Global Banking placement closed on 30 September and allows one UK and one rest-of-EMEA application per year. Lazard's 2027–28 placement opened on 22 September with one London application per year ([UBS](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/careers/early-careers/industrial-placement.html); [Lazard](https://www.lazard.com/about-lazard/locations/united-kingdom/careers-in-the-united-kingdom/early-career-programmes-in-the-uk/)). **Off-cycle internships** of three to six months run at Barclays, Rothschild & Co, Evercore and UBS ([Barclays](https://search.jobs.barclays/internships); [Rothschild & Co](https://www.rothschildandco.com/en/careers/students-and-graduates/opportunities/)). Some are disguised graduate routes: BofA's 2027 UK & Ireland off-cycle analyst role requires availability to join the graduate programme from July 2027 ([BofA Careers](https://careers.bankofamerica.com/en-us/students/job-detail/14592/investment-banking-2027-off-cycle-analyst-uk-ireland-investment-banking-emea-corporate-broking-london-london-united-kingdom)). On the continent, off-cycle is the main format. Paris, Frankfurt, Milan and Amsterdam post roles **three to six months before the start date** ([AnalystClass](https://analystclass.com/internships)). French placements typically last six months, start in January and are timed to the *césure* gap year ([Fyntraq](https://fyntraq.io/investment-banking-internships-europe)). Nomura Paris allows one application per recruitment year ([Nomura Campus](https://nomuracampus.tal.net/vx/mobile-0/appcentre-ext/brand-4/candidate/so/pm/1/pl/1/opp/1202-2026-Investment-Banking-Off-Cycle-Internship-Paris-January-start/en-GB)). Language is the gate: Italian is required "at every employer we verified" in Milan, and Frankfurt reportedly tolerates English for internships but expects German for full-time roles ([AnalystClass](https://analystclass.com/internships); [M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-recruiting-europe/)). Spring weeks do not exist in the US, so continental European students usually travel to London for them ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/)).

### Access programmes, visas and the target-school question

If you are eligible, apply to access programmes in the same October–November window as bank applications. **10,000 Black Interns** takes applications until **1 November 2026** for paid summer 2027 placements, but it requires an **unrestricted right to work full-time in the UK**, which excludes Student-visa holders ([Opportunities for Youth](https://opportunitiesforyouth.org/2026/10/07/10000-black-interns-programme-2027-paid-uk-internships-for-black-students-and-graduates/)). **SEO London** runs "Spring Into Finance" in October 2026 and spring weeks from March to July 2027 ([SEO London](https://www.seo-london.org/spring-into-programmes)). **upReach's Aspiring Professionals Programme** requires at least two socio-economic criteria, such as being first in your family at university or having a household income under £45,000 ([upReach](https://cms.upreach.org.uk/uploads/Programme_Descriptions_Sector_Springboards_Banking_and_Finance_05ea1bfccf.pdf)). Bright Network's virtual Internship Experience UK runs each June ([Bright Network](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/ieuk-2026/)).

International students have three rules to plan around. A Student visa allows up to **20 hours a week in term time but full-time work in official vacations**, so Easter spring weeks and summer internships generally fit, while term-time off-cycle roles can breach the cap ([UKCISA](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/Information--Advice/Working/Student-work)). The **Graduate Route falls from 24 to 18 months for applications made on or after 1 January 2027** ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/shorter-graduate-route-from-january-2027-uk-govt-confirms/)). Skilled Worker sponsorship requires **£41,700** in general or about **£33,400 at the new-entrant rate** ([Jobbatical](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/uk-skilled-worker-visa-salary-threshold-employer-guide); [Rowan](https://withrowan.co.uk/guides/new-entrant-salary)). London analyst pay clears both comfortably, so the real barrier is whether the bank will sponsor that particular role, and sponsorship is role-dependent even at licensed sponsors ([CVailor](https://cvailor.com/blog/investment-banking-jobs-visa-sponsorship-uk)). The core UK "targets" are commonly listed as Oxford, Cambridge, LSE, Imperial, UCL and Warwick, with Durham, Bristol, Nottingham, Bath, KCL, Edinburgh, St Andrews and Exeter as semi-targets ([Investment Banking Analysts](https://investmentbankinganalysts.com/best-uk-universities-for-investment-banking-top-target-schools-for-city-jobs/); [WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/regular-and-low-semi-target-universities-in-the-uk)). This is practitioner consensus, not bank data. Hiring at the application stage is broader than the lists imply. Spring weeks are the best lever for non-target students because they are easier to win than summer internships and convert without needing a target-school brand.

### Know the job, the pay and the exits before you claim to want them

Analysts build Excel models and PowerPoint pitchbooks, research companies and turn around comments from senior bankers, and a large share of the day is spent waiting for those comments ([The Forage](https://www.theforage.com/blog/careers/investment-banking-hours)). The main divisions are coverage/M&A, ECM, DCM, leveraged finance and restructuring, organised into sector groups (FIG, TMT, healthcare, industrials, natural resources) and product groups. Hours commonly run 70–90+ a week. After the 2024 death of a BofA associate, J.P. Morgan capped junior hours at **80 a week "in most cases", with live deals exempt** ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2024/09/12/jpmorgan-cap-junior-bankers-hours)). A secondary source reports juniors still averaging about 78 hours in 2025 ([ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/investment-banking-hours-lifestyle)), and no London-specific cap was found.

On pay, guides cluster first-year bulge-bracket **base salaries at about £60,000–£70,000**, with **total pay of roughly £75,000–£120,000** depending on the guide and the bonus year. Elite boutiques pay a premium that widens from the second year ([Canary Wharfian](https://www.canarywharfian.co.uk/investment-banking/634/investment-banking-salary-guide-2026-londoneurope); [PrepLounge](https://www.preplounge.com/en/blog/finance/investment-banking/salary/uk)). Every one of these figures is a commercial estimate. The last rigorous survey, based on 2023 pay, found US entry-level pay **57% higher** than the UK ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-05/wall-street-bonus-and-salary-finance-workers-make-more-in-us-than-uk-asia)), and current estimates of the London discount range from 5–10% to 50% (**contested**; [M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banker-salary/)). Self-reported intern pay at individual banks centres on annualised medians of about £48k–£70k, which works out at roughly £10k–£14k for ten weeks by simple pro-rating ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salaries/london-investment-banking-summer-analyst-intern-salary-SRCH_IL.0,6_IM1035_KO7,47.htm)). The classic exit is private equity after about 18 months. In London that recruiting is headhunter-led, with interviews about three to four months before start dates ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/london-private-equity-recruiting)). US "on-cycle" PE recruiting paused in summer 2025 after Jamie Dimon threatened to fire analysts who accepted future-dated PE jobs, then restarted abruptly in January 2026 ([Vault](https://legacy.vault.com/blogs/in-the-black-vaults-finance-careers-blog/private-equity-on-cycle-hiring-is-faster-earlier-and-wilder-than-ever); [Private Equity Wire](https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/private-equity-firms-restart-delayed-graduate-hiring-after-recruitment-backlash/)). That is one reason never to signal an exit plan in interviews.

## Four filters stand between your CV and a banker

London selection usually runs in this order: written application, online assessment, recorded video interview, then a final assessment centre or superday. Each stage screens out a different kind of weakness, and humans are increasingly reserved for the later stages.

### The written application is the first cut

Expect a CV plus either a cover letter of about 300–500 words or one to five motivational questions of about 150–300 words each. Some banks ask for both ([trymerra.ai](https://www.trymerra.ai/blog/spring-week-interview-prep-2026-uk-investment-banking-insight-week-guide); [Lancaster University](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/lums/our-departments/accounting-and-finance/blogs/investment-banking-spring-weeks)). Glassdoor reports describe Morgan Stanley's spring process as CV and cover letter, then a psychometric test, HireVue and a phone interview ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Morgan-Stanley-Spring-Week-Interview-Questions-EI_IE2282.0,14_KO15,26.htm)). A 2026 guide says Goldman asks for a **300-word motivational statement** before its HireVue ([OurGen](https://ourgen.uk/insights/goldman-sachs-spring-week)). UK guides warn that on some systems a weak written answer ends an application before a human opens the CV ([Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-application-guide)). With acceptance rates near 0.7%, generic answers do not survive, and the most common errors are giving no division preference, reusing an unchanged CV and missing early openings ([Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-application-guide)).

### One page, grades first, British conventions

A first-year CV longer than one page is described as "a screen-out at effectively every bank". Put education first, with your predicted or actual classification and A-level (or equivalent) grades, use British spelling and day-month-year dates, and leave out the photo ([Calibre CV](https://calibrecv.co.uk/blog/spring-week-cv-guide)). A-levels "carry far more weight at spring week stage than at any later point". A part-time job, a society committee role and a sport are "perfectly adequate raw material" if each bullet is quantified ([Calibre CV](https://calibrecv.co.uk/blog/spring-week-cv-guide); [Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-cv-guide)). The first read is brutally short. The Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking study measured an average **7.4-second** initial screen and found that single-column layouts performed best ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)), while banking guides suggest 20–30 seconds. Use a single column with Education, then Experience, then Leadership & Activities, then Skills, Languages & Interests. Keep every bullet to one line, and include two or three specific interests a banker could start a conversation about.

## The technical curriculum, taught in pounds and IFRS

How much technical depth you need depends on the stage. Spring-week interviews are mostly behavioural, with minimal or no technical questions, though spring weeks themselves often include accounting and Excel training ([F&E Training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/careers-in-finance/investment-banking-careers-spring-week-internships)). Summer second rounds and superdays are where core technicals get tested, and modelling tests are mostly reserved for later rounds, off-cycle roles, lateral hires and PE ([TargetJobs](https://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/accountancy-banking-and-finance/example-investment-banking-interview); [Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/leveraged-buyout-lbo-modeling-1-hour-practice-test)). The nine lessons below take you from zero to internship-ready. Every £ worked example is illustrative arithmetic built from the cited formulas, assumes the **UK corporation tax main rate of 25%** (in force since April 2023 for profits over £250,000; [Charlton Baker](https://charltonbaker.co.uk/news-blog/corporation-tax-changes-from-april-2023)), and was checked numerically.

| Stage | Technical bar (synthesis of sources) |
|---|---|
| Spring week | Explain the three statements and how they link; name the main valuation methods; know EV vs equity value; basic Excel |
| Summer internship (IBD) | Lessons 1–7 verbally: statement walk-throughs, the EV bridge, "walk me through a DCF", WACC, multiples, accretion/dilution, paper LBO; build a simple three-statement model in 60–90 minutes |
| Off-cycle / full-time / PE | Full three-statement model with a debt schedule, DCF with sensitivities, comps with calendarisation, merger model, a 45–60-minute LBO test, fast pitchbook formatting |

### Lesson 1: net income is the hinge of the three statements

The income statement (IS) ends in net income. Net income opens the indirect cash flow statement (CFS), and after dividends it flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet (BS). Non-cash charges such as depreciation and amortisation (D&A) and working-capital movements reconcile net income to cash from operations. Capex adds to PP&E and appears in cash from investing, debt drives interest on the IS and issuance or repayment in cash from financing, and the net change in cash closes back into the BS ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/how-are-the-financial-statements-linked-together/); [F&E Training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/financial-modeling/linking-three-financial-statements)). The classic test is a depreciation increase. Net income falls by D × (1 − t) and cash rises by D × t, the tax shield, provided the depreciation is tax-deductible. Interviewers expect you to **state your tax-rate assumption first** ([Wall Street Prep](https://wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/accounting-interview-questions); [BIWS](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/kb/accounting/depreciation-on-the-3-financial-statements)).

| Statement | Line | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| IS | Depreciation | +£10.00 | Given |
| IS | Pre-tax profit | −£10.00 | Expense up |
| IS | Tax | −£2.50 | 25% × £10 |
| IS | **Net income** | **−£7.50** | £10 × (1 − 25%) |
| CFS | Net income, then add back D&A | −£7.50, +£10.00 | Depreciation is non-cash |
| CFS | **Net change in cash** | **+£2.50** | Tax shield |
| BS | Cash / PP&E | +£2.50 / −£10.00 | Total assets −£7.50 |
| BS | Retained earnings | −£7.50 | Balances with assets |

Two follow-ups separate strong candidates. First, if only *book* depreciation rises (say a useful-life estimate changes) while *tax* depreciation does not, the £2.50 lower tax charge is a non-cash deferred-tax movement, so cash is unchanged. This follows from the temporary-difference logic in which a deferred tax liability equals the book–tax difference × tax rate ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/deferred-tax-liability-dtl/); [CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/deferred-tax-liability-asset)). Second, in the UK, accounting depreciation is generally replaced for tax purposes by capital allowances. This research did not verify that point, so in an interview say "assuming book and tax depreciation are equal". Drill the other standard walk-throughs the same way, always stating your assumptions aloud.

| Transaction (25% tax) | Income statement | Cash flow statement | Balance sheet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy £100 of inventory with cash | No effect | CFO −£100 (inventory up) | Cash −£100, inventory +£100 |
| Sell that inventory for £150 on credit, tax accrued | Revenue +150, COGS −100, tax −12.5, **NI +£37.50** | NI +37.5, inventory +100, receivables −150, tax payable +12.5: **CFO £0** | Assets +£50 (receivables +150, inventory −100); tax payable +12.5, retained earnings +37.5 |
| £50 goodwill impairment (not tax-deductible) | **NI −£50**, no tax shield | Add back +£50: cash unchanged | Goodwill −£50, retained earnings −£50 |
| Raise £100 debt at 6%, hold one year | Interest −£6, tax +£1.50, **NI −£4.50** | US GAAP: CFO −£4.50. **IFRS 18 (from 2027):** CFO +£1.50 (lower tax), CFF −£6 interest; net still −£4.50 | Cash and retained earnings each −£4.50 (after the initial +£100 cash and debt) |

Working capital for valuation means operating current assets minus operating current liabilities (at its narrowest, receivables plus inventory minus payables). It excludes cash and debt because those are financing items, and an increase reduces free cash flow ([CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/knowledge/finance/what-is-net-working-capital)).

### Lesson 2: four IFRS points that mark you out as a London candidate

Most interview-prep material (BIWS, WSP, Vault) is built on US GAAP. UK and European companies report under IFRS, and four differences change the numbers you will see.

**IFRS 16 leases.** Since 2019, lessees have recognised a right-of-use asset and a lease liability for virtually all leases. The only optional exemptions are short-term leases of 12 months or less and low-value assets, guided at roughly $5,000 when new. US GAAP (ASC 842) has no low-value exemption ([GAAP Dynamics](https://www.gaapdynamics.com/insights/blog/2019/10/01/ifrs-16-vs-asc-842-how-to-account-for-low-value-leases/)). Under IFRS 16 the lease cost splits into depreciation and interest, both below EBITDA, so **reported EBITDA is higher**. US operating leases keep a single straight-line expense above EBITDA ([asset.accountant](https://www.asset.accountant/blog/asc-842-vs-ifrs-16/)). FTI Consulting's rule is to count lease liabilities as net debt when EBITDA excludes lease costs, and otherwise to use **EBITDAaL** (EBITDA after leases). Pre-2019 precedents need lease adjustments, and the effect is large for airlines, logistics, telecom towers and healthcare ([FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/uk/insights/articles/valuation-implications-ifrs-16)).

**IFRS 18 cash-flow classification.** Under US GAAP, interest paid is an operating cash flow ([Deloitte DART](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/presentation/asc230-10/roadmap-statement-cash-flow/appendix-e-differences-between-us-gaap/appendix-e-differences-between-us-gaap)). Current IAS 7 lets companies choose between operating and financing. **For annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027**, IFRS 18 removes the choice for most corporates: interest paid and dividends paid go to financing, while interest and dividends received go to investing. The indirect method will start from IFRS 18's new "operating profit or loss" subtotal ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/ifrs-18-changes-statement-cash-flows.html); [IAS Plus](https://www.iasplus.com/standard/ias07.htm)). Its UK endorsement status was not verified for this research. The change takes effect in the middle of the cycle you are recruiting in, so a candidate who explains it unprompted sounds current.

**Asset accounting differences.** IFRS prohibits LIFO, capitalises development costs once its criteria are met (IAS 38), allows impairment reversals for assets other than goodwill, and permits a revaluation model for PP&E ([FitSmallBusiness](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/gaap-vs-ifrs/)).

**Goodwill.** Under IFRS, goodwill is never amortised. It is tested annually for impairment at the level of the cash-generating unit, against the higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use, and the impairment can never be reversed ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2022/goodwill-impairment-ifrs-standards-us-gaap.html); [ICAEW](https://icaew.com/technical/corporate%20reporting/ifrs/ifrs%20standards/ias%2036%20impairment%20of%20assets)). Private UK mid-market targets reporting under UK GAAP (FRS 102) follow different goodwill rules, which this research did not cover.

### Lesson 3: every EV bridge item exists to match the denominator

Equity value is what belongs to ordinary shareholders: diluted shares × share price. Enterprise value (EV) is the value of the operating business to all capital providers. Each bridge adjustment keeps the numerator consistent with what EBITDA captures. You add non-controlling interests because consolidated EBITDA includes 100% of subsidiaries. You subtract associates because their earnings sit outside EBITDA. You add pension deficits as debt-like items, sometimes tax-effected by bank convention ([Wall Street Prep](https://wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/equity-value-to-enterprise-value-bridge); [F&E Training](https://fe.training/?p=10001242); [ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/valuation-investment-banking/pensions-off-balance-sheet-hidden-liabilities)). Diluted shares use the treasury stock method: in-the-money options are assumed exercised and the proceeds buy back shares at the current price ([CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/treasury-stock-method/)). Take a UK plc at £5.00 a share with 100m basic shares and 10m options struck at £3.00. Exercise raises £30m, which buys back 6m shares, so diluted shares are **104m** and equity value is **£520m**.

| Bridge item (£m) | Amount | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Equity value (diluted) | 520 | 104m × £5.00 |
| + Borrowings | 200 | Debt claim |
| + IFRS 16 lease liabilities | 80 | EBITDA is post-IFRS 16, so lease cost is excluded from it |
| + Preference shares | 20 | Senior claim |
| + Non-controlling interests | 30 | EBITDA consolidates 100% of subsidiaries |
| + Pension deficit (pre-tax) | 25 | Debt-like item (£18.75m if tax-effected) |
| − Cash | (70) | Non-operating asset |
| − Investments in associates | (40) | Their earnings are not in EBITDA |
| **Enterprise value** | **765** | On £85m EBITDA = **9.0x** |

The consistency trap matters for IFRS companies. If you strip the £80m of leases out of EV (£685m) but still divide by pre-lease EBITDA, the multiple falls to an understated 8.1x. The correct alternative divides £685m by EBITDAaL (£75m after an assumed £10m of lease cash cost), which gives 9.1x. The trick questions all follow from the identity EV = equity + net debt + other claims. Raising £100m of debt, or issuing £100m of shares, and holding the cash leaves EV unchanged. Borrowing to buy back shares leaves EV unchanged. Paying a dividend reduces cash and equity together, so EV is unchanged. Spending £100m of cash on a factory raises measured EV by £100m, because value moves from a non-operating asset into operations. EV can be negative when cash exceeds market capitalisation plus debt. Market equity value cannot be negative, but book equity can. EV pairs with pre-interest metrics (EBITDA, EBIT, revenue), and equity value pairs with post-interest metrics (net income, EPS). Unlevered free cash flow discounted at WACC gives EV, while levered free cash flow discounted at the cost of equity gives equity value directly ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/top-10-investment-banking-interview-questions)).

### Lesson 4: four valuation methods, one football field

The canonical methods, as framed in Rosenbaum & Pearl's *Investment Banking*, are trading comparables, precedent transactions, DCF and LBO, presented together as ranges on a "football field" ([O'Reilly](https://oreilly.com/library/view/investment-banking-3rd/9781119867876)). "Which method gives the highest value?" is a staple London question, asked at Rothschild among others. The answer is usually **precedent transactions, because they embed a 20–40% control premium and often synergies**. Trading comps reflect minority-stake pricing and usually come out lowest. A DCF typically falls in between but can top the range on aggressive assumptions ([CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/learn/resources/valuation/precedent-transaction-analysis/); [ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/valuation-investment-banking/which-valuation-method-highest-lowest-value)). The LBO shows what a financial sponsor could pay, which often acts as a floor. Sum-of-the-parts suits groups with unrelated segments and spin-off analysis, sometimes with a conglomerate discount of about 10–15% whose existence is debated ([AnalystPrep](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/sum-of-parts-valuation/)).

The DCF rests on four formulas, each verified across several sources.

| Building block | Formula | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Unlevered free cash flow | **UFCF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A − capex − ΔNWC** | [Wall Street Prep](https://wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/unlevered-free-cash-flow) |
| Cost of capital | **WACC = E/V × Re + D/V × Rd × (1 − t)**, market-value weights; CAPM **Re = Rf + β × ERP** | [CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/learn/resources/valuation/what-is-wacc-formula) |
| Beta | **Relevered β = unlevered β × [1 + (1 − t) × D/E]**; Damodaran's bottom-up method unlevers peer betas at their own D/E, averages them, and relevers at the target's D/E | [Damodaran, NYU Stern](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/podcasts/valspr22/session6slides.pdf) |
| Terminal value | **Gordon TV = FCF_N × (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g)** with g below WACC, cross-checked against an exit-multiple TV; TV is commonly **60–80% of EV** | [BVResources](https://www.bvresources.com/articles/bvwire/avoid-this-inconsistency-with-the-terminal-value) |

The illustrative £ case assumes revenue of £1,000m growing 5% a year, a 15% EBIT margin, D&A at 4% of revenue, capex at 5%, and NWC investment equal to 10% of each year's revenue increase. Relevering an unlevered beta of 0.90 at 25% D/E gives 1.069. A 4.5% risk-free rate stands in for the 10-year gilt yield and the equity risk premium (ERP) is 5.25%; both are placeholders, so check live figures before using them. A 6% pre-tax cost of debt becomes 4.5% after tax. Together these give a cost of equity of 10.1% and a **WACC of about 9.0%**.

| £m | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,050.0 | 1,102.5 | 1,157.6 | 1,215.5 | 1,276.3 |
| EBIT (15%) | 157.5 | 165.4 | 173.6 | 182.3 | 191.4 |
| NOPAT (× 0.75) | 118.1 | 124.0 | 130.2 | 136.7 | 143.6 |
| + D&A − capex − ΔNWC | −15.5 | −16.3 | −17.1 | −17.9 | −18.8 |
| **UFCF** | **102.6** | **107.8** | **113.1** | **118.8** | **124.7** |
| PV at 9% (year-end) | 94.2 | 90.7 | 87.4 | 84.2 | 81.1 |

The explicit cash flows are worth **£437.5m**. With 2% growth, the Gordon terminal value is £1,817.7m, worth **£1,181.4m** today, so **EV is £1,618.8m** and the terminal value is **73% of it**. The terminal value implies a 7.5x multiple of year-5 EBITDA (£242.5m). An 8.0x exit multiple would give an EV of £1,698.3m and an implied perpetual growth rate of 2.4%, a plausible cross-check. Subtracting £300m of net debt leaves equity of £1,318.8m, or **£13.19 a share** on 100m shares.

The mid-year convention discounts each year's cash flow at t − 0.5. That lifts the explicit flows by about 4.4% here, which matches the typical 3–5% uplift ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/mid-year-convention/)). Practitioners disagree on how to discount the terminal value under this convention. F&E argues an exit-multiple terminal value is a sale at the end of year N and should be discounted the full N periods, while others shift a Gordon terminal value to N − 0.5 ([Felix, F&E Training](https://felix.fe.training/video-lesson/lesson/dcf-valuation/?video=1142); [BVResources](https://www.bvresources.com/articles/bvwire/avoid-this-inconsistency-with-the-terminal-value)). Sensitivity shows why the terminal value dominates the debate. Narrowing WACC − g from 7% to 6% raises the Gordon terminal value by 16.7%. For a £ DCF, use gilts for the risk-free rate, a UK or European ERP (Damodaran publishes country ERPs), and the 25% UK tax rate. Under IFRS 16, choose one lease treatment: either leases are debt (lease liabilities in net debt, lease cash costs left out of UFCF) or leases are operating costs (deducted in UFCF, liabilities excluded). Doing both double-counts.

### Lesson 5: multiples must pair numerator with denominator, and financials break the rules

Banks, insurers and REITs are valued on equity multiples because, for them, debt and interest are part of the operating business.

| Multiple | Use |
|---|---|
| EV/Revenue | Early-stage, unprofitable, high-growth companies |
| EV/EBITDA | Default for most corporates (but it ignores capex) |
| EV/EBITDAaL | IFRS 16 lease-heavy sectors: retail, airlines, telecoms ([FTI](https://www.fticonsulting.com/uk/insights/articles/valuation-implications-ifrs-16)) |
| EV/EBIT | Capital-intensive businesses |
| P/E | Mature profitable companies; meaningless with negative earnings |
| P/TBV | Banks, read against return on tangible equity and cost of equity |
| P/FFO, P/AFFO, P/NAV | REITs |
| P/EV (embedded value) | Life insurers ([Actuarial Post/Milliman](https://actuarialpost.co.uk/article/milliman-reviews-embedded-value-of-major-european-insurers-6491.htm)) |

For banks, the justified **P/TBV ≈ (ROE − g) ÷ (COE − g)**. A bank earning 15% with a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth deserves about **1.7x** tangible book ([BIWS](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/kb/bank-modeling/price-to-book-value/)). A UK bank earning a 12% return on tangible equity against an 11% cost of equity with 2% growth deserves only about 1.11x. That is the intuition behind "a bank trades above book only if it earns more than its cost of equity". For REITs, funds from operations (FFO) = net income + real-estate D&A − gains on property sales, and NAV is built from net operating income ÷ cap rate, less debt ([AnalystPrep](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/reit-share-value-calculation-using-net-asset-value-p-ffo-p-affo-and-discounted-cash-flow-approaches/)). UK property companies are often discussed against EPRA net tangible assets rather than US-style FFO, a point this research did not verify.

### Lesson 6: accretion and dilution is a yield comparison

A merger model asks whether pro forma EPS rises or falls. The shortcut compares the target's earnings yield at the offer price with the acquirer's cost of each funding source. For stock, the cost is the acquirer's earnings yield (1 ÷ its P/E). For new debt, it is the interest rate × (1 − t), and for cash, the forgone interest × (1 − t). An all-stock deal is accretive when the acquirer's P/E exceeds the target's P/E **at the offer price**, ignoring synergies and fees ([BIWS](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/kb/ma-and-merger-models/eps-accretion-dilution/); [Intervyo](https://www.intervyo.co.uk/technicals/accretion-dilution)). The worked example uses an acquirer at £20.00 with 100m shares and £100m of net income (EPS £1.00, P/E 20x, a 5.0% earnings yield). It bids a 30% premium for a target at £10.00 with 50m shares and £30m of net income. The offer is £13.00 a share, or **£650m**, an offer P/E of 21.7x and an earnings yield of **4.62%**.

| Funding | Pro forma NI (£m) | Shares (m) | PF EPS | Accretion/(dilution) | Shortcut check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% stock (32.5m new shares) | 130.0 | 132.5 | £0.981 | **(1.9%)** | 5.0% cost > 4.62% yield |
| 100% new debt at 6% | 100.75 | 100.0 | £1.008 | **+0.75%** | 4.5% < 4.62% |
| 100% existing cash (3% forgone) | 115.4 | 100.0 | £1.154 | **+15.4%** | 2.25% < 4.62% |
| 50% stock / 50% debt | 115.4 | 116.25 | £0.992 | **(0.75%)** | Blended 4.75% > 4.62% |
| All stock + £10m pre-tax synergies | 137.5 | 132.5 | £1.038 | **+3.8%** | Synergies flip it |

The all-stock deal breaks even with **£3.33m of pre-tax synergies**. Purchase price allocation then bites. Goodwill = purchase price − fair value of identifiable net assets, after asset write-ups and the deferred tax liability those write-ups create ([Wall Street Prep](https://wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/purchase-price-allocation-ppa); [BIWS](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/kb/ma-and-merger-models/purchase-price-allocation/)). With £250m of target book equity and a £100m intangible write-up, the DTL is £25m, identifiable net assets are £325m and **goodwill is £325m**. Under IFRS the goodwill is not amortised, but the £100m write-up is. Over ten years that costs £7.5m a year after tax, which deepens the all-stock dilution from 1.9% to about **7.5%**. Buyers pay premiums for synergies, control, auction tension and scarcity, but the deal only creates value if the present value of synergies, net of integration costs, exceeds the premium. The "winner's curse" is the risk that the winning bidder is the one that overestimated most ([AnalystPrep](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/effects-of-price-and-payment-method/)). Paying cash signals confidence in the synergies, while paying in stock shares synergy risk with the target's holders and may signal that the buyer thinks its own shares are fully valued. A London M&A candidate should also study the UK Takeover Code mechanics: firm offers, cash confirmation, schemes of arrangement versus contractual offers, and squeeze-outs. This research did not verify them, so learn them from UK legal sources.

### Lesson 7: a paper LBO in under ten minutes

A sponsor buys at an entry multiple, funds the purchase mostly with debt, uses free cash flow to repay debt, and exits after three to seven years. Returns come from **EBITDA growth, debt paydown and multiple expansion**, and base cases usually assume no expansion. Memorise the five-year rules of thumb: **2.0x ≈ 15% IRR, 2.5x ≈ 20%, 3.0x ≈ 25%** ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/paper-lbo); [F&E Training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/private-equity/paper-lbo/)). The illustrative case buys £100m of EBITDA at 10.0x, funded with 6.0x debt (£600m at 7%) and a **£400m equity cheque**. EBITDA grows £6m a year, D&A equals capex at £20m, and all free cash flow sweeps to debt.

| £m | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EBITDA | 106.0 | 112.0 | 118.0 | 124.0 | 130.0 |
| Interest (7% × opening debt) | (42.0) | (39.7) | (36.9) | (33.7) | (30.0) |
| Net income = cash to repay debt | 33.0 | 39.2 | 45.8 | 52.7 | 60.0 |
| Closing debt | 567.0 | 527.8 | 482.0 | 429.3 | 369.3 |

Exit at 10.0x × £130m = £1,300m, less £369.3m of debt, leaves **£930.7m of equity**. That is a **2.33x MOIC and an 18.4% IRR**. The £530.7m gain splits 57% from EBITDA growth (£30m × 10x) and 43% from debt paydown. The exit multiple dominates sensitivity: 9.0x gives 2.00x and 14.9%, while 11.0x gives 2.65x and 21.5%. Exact calculation confirms the rules of thumb (2.0x over five years is 14.9%, 3.0x is 24.6%) and shows that holding period matters: 2.0x over three years is 26.0%. Fees matter too. Adding £25m of fees to sources and uses lifts the equity cheque to £425m and cuts the IRR to about 17.0%, which is why omitting fees overstates returns ([A Simple Model](https://www.asimplemodel.com/insights/sources-and-uses)). A good LBO candidate has stable, recurring cash flows to service debt, which is why cyclical businesses deleverage poorly ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/deleveraging/)). It also needs low capex, operational levers, a defensible market position and a credible exit. To work backwards to a target, a 20% IRR over five years needs about 2.5x, or about £1,000m of exit equity, which here means about 10.5x on exit. That is the multiple expansion you would have to believe in.

### Lesson 8: London capital markets mechanics

A Main Market IPO typically takes six months or more. It opens with an **Intention to Float** announcement, which is market practice rather than a legal requirement. Under FCA research rules the ITF has typically been preceded by an "expectation of intention to float" so that unconnected analysts can publish research, although the FCA was reported to be considering scrapping this two-stage system, with no outcome confirmed ([MoneyAM/Bloomberg](https://old.moneyam.com/action/news/showArticle?id=7375308)). An FCA-approved prospectus and a price range follow, then about **two weeks of bookbuilding and roadshow**, then pricing and allocation. Stabilisation runs through a **15% greenshoe**, and **180-day lock-ups** are typical for selling shareholders ([LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/intention-to-float-announcements-in-main-market-aim-ipos); [Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/ipos-in-the-uk-whats-involved/); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.latham.london/2019/06/cornerstone-investments-and-private-equity-sponsored-ipo-exits/)). The UK prospectus regime was reformed on **19 January 2026**, sharply reducing when an approved prospectus is needed for further issues. The source attribution for this was unclear, so verify it on the FCA's site. European IPO gross spreads have historically run around 3–5% against the US "7% solution", according to studies dated 2003–2018 ([Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:cf406437-7ba7-4fec-aa1a-2425e86b426f/files/m1b5a7f71847b307e8865a2a7b066dea8)).

Follow-on equity raises in the UK are mostly placings and accelerated bookbuilds. The Pre-Emption Group's 2022 Principles allow companies to disapply pre-emption rights for **up to 10% for general purposes plus 10% for an acquisition or capital investment, plus up to 2% for a follow-on retail offer** ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2022/11/new-peg-statement-of-principles-on-the-disapplication-of-pre-emption-rights)). Rights issues are priced against the theoretical ex-rights price, **TERP = (existing shares × price + new shares × issue price) ÷ total shares** ([Sharescope](https://www.sharescope.co.uk/philoakley_article45.jsp)). A 1-for-4 rights issue at 300p on a 400p share gives a TERP of 380p, a nil-paid right worth 80p, and a 21% discount to TERP.

A convertible bond is a straight bond plus a call option on the issuer's equity, struck at a **10–50% conversion premium** ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/events/2020/07/convertible-bonds--understanding-the-key-benefits.pdf)). At £10 with a 30% premium, a £100,000 bond converts into 7,692 shares, and conversion only makes sense above £13.

On the debt side, investment grade means **BBB−/Baa3 or better**, with light documentation. High-yield bonds carry fixed coupons, call protection and *incurrence* covenants. Leveraged loans float over SONIA, EURIBOR or SOFR, and cov-lite loans grew from just over 10% of the market in 2007 to more than 80% by 2020 ([Boston Fed](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/research-department-working-paper/2022/high-yield-debt-covenants-and-their-real-effects); [Marquette](https://www.marquetteassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Were-Not-So-Different-High-Yield-Bonds-and-Leveraged-Loans.pdf)). Private-credit **unitranche** loans, sized from about €10m to €2bn, now compete with both ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/-/media/files/publications/2020/11/unitranche-financing/unitranche_financingan_introduction.pdf)).

### Lesson 9: Excel fluency, modelling tests and the resources worth paying for

Institutional modelling tests are usually an integrated three-statement model or an LBO. They range from a 15-minute paper exercise to a three-hour Excel build ([Intervyo](https://www.intervyo.co.uk/assessments/excel-modelling-test)). A standard LBO test runs about 45–60 minutes, and associate-level PE tests about 90 minutes ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/leveraged-buyout-lbo-modeling-1-hour-practice-test); [F&E Training](https://www.fe.training/?p=10088604)). Build keyboard-driven Excel (XLOOKUP or INDEX/MATCH, SUMIFS, data tables) and clean pitchbook formatting. Macabacus offers free training on the add-in that many banks use ([Macabacus](https://macabacus.com/on-demand-training)).

A strong low-cost stack starts with Rosenbaum & Pearl's *Investment Banking* (3rd edition, with six downloadable model templates) as the core text ([O'Reilly](https://oreilly.com/library/view/investment-banking-3rd/9781119867876)). Add the *Vault Guide to Finance Interviews*, often free through university careers services ([Cornell](https://career.cornell.edu/resources/vault-guide-to-finance-interviews-9th-edition/)), Damodaran's free courses and datasets on betas, ERPs and multiples ([Damodaran Online](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/home.htm)), and A Simple Model's free three-statement, DCF and LBO lessons ([A Simple Model](https://www.asimplemodel.com/resources/)). On paid courses, Wall Street Oasis sentiment says "BIWS is much more analogous to questions you'll get in interviews" and recommends WSP for learning to model. The consensus is that certificates build knowledge rather than strengthen a CV ([WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/wsp-vs-wall-street-training-vs-biws-vs-nyif-investment-banking)). Employer regard for CFI's FMVA is disputed, and the main dissent comes from a competitor ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/wall-street-prep-vs-corporate-finance-institute/)). Training The Street mainly appears as training that banks provide after you join ([TTS](https://trainingthestreet.com/about/)). Buy at most one paid course. Whichever you use, add the UK layer yourself: IFRS 16 and 18, the 25% tax rate, gilts, the Takeover Code and UK ECM practice. A realistic part-time sequence takes **10–14 weeks**: accounting (weeks 1–3), EV and multiples (4–5), comps, precedents and DCF (6–8), M&A (9–10), LBO (11–12), then capital markets, sector multiples and mock interviews (13–14).

## Commercial awareness in 2026: value up, volume down, Britain for sale

UK employers test commercial awareness as applied reasoning, not recall of headlines. They want to know whether you understand how business change affects the firm and its clients ([Bright Network](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/graduate-career-advice/what-is-commercial-awareness/); [Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/commercial-awareness-markets-guide)). For autumn 2026 the story is coherent: **more value from fewer deals, a run of takeovers of UK plcs, a London IPO market that barely functions, and interest rates turning against leveraged buyers**.

### Europe's deal value hit multi-year highs as deal counts fell

European M&A reached about **US$661.5bn in H1 2026, the highest half-year since 2021** and already 85% of 2025's full-year total, while **deal volume fell 17.8%** half-on-half. Strategic buyers led, with defence, renewables and EVs prominent ([A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/global-ma-insights/european-ma-maintains-momentum-amid-broader-market-uncertainty)). EMEA as a whole had its strongest first half in 19 years at US$676bn ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/european-union/corporate-commercial-law/european-ma-maintains-momentum-amid-broader-market-uncertainty)). The UK was Europe's largest market by deal count in Q1 2026 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2026/05/europe-m-and-a-by-the-numbers-q1-2026)). The headline UK figures **conflict**. Reuters reported foreign takeover offers for UK companies of more than **US$231bn** by early July, up 210% ([Cyprus Mail/Reuters](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/07/05/foreign-takeover-offers-for-uk-companies-hit-231-billion-in-2026)), while Bloomberg's September headline said UK takeovers had "raced past $100 billion" ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/uk-takeovers-race-past-100-billion-after-summer-deals-surge)). The gap probably reflects all approaches, including rejected ones, versus agreed deals, so cite the definition along with the number. CNBC's 1 October framing was "Bargain Britain lures foreign buyers as hostile takeovers surge" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/01/hostile-takeovers-mergers-acquisitions-uk.html)). Financial services was the busiest corner. EY counted 135 UK financial-services deals in H1 2026 against 108 a year earlier, with disclosed value up from **£4.2bn to £33.7bn**, led by wealth and asset management ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_uk/newsroom/2026/07/ey-financial-services-m-and-a-analysis)). Momentum then slowed globally in Q3 after a "record-chasing first half" ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/global-ma-momentum-slows-in-3q26-after-record-chasing-first-half-302895987.html)). A Hogan Lovells partner calls the UK public market "bifurcated": big deals at the top, most activity below £250m ([LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/insights/trends-in-uk-public-m-a-deals-in-h1-2026/index.html)).

### League tables by value and by count describe two different businesses

GlobalData's H1 2026 European adviser table by **value** reads Morgan Stanley (US$128.3bn), Goldman Sachs, Barclays, BofA, J.P. Morgan. By **deal count** it reads Rothschild & Co (48 deals), Houlihan Lokey (45), then KPMG and PwC (44 each) and EY (40) ([Private Banker International](https://www.privatebankerinternational.com/news/europe-ma-advisers-h1-2026/)). That split is a ready-made interview insight. Bulge brackets dominate large-cap mandates, while independent advisers and the Big 4 do real M&A work in the mid-market. It also answers "why Rothschild?" or "why Houlihan Lokey?" with data rather than adjectives. Other providers such as LSEG, Dealogic and Mergermarket rank differently, so name GlobalData if you quote these.

### London's listings debate is now a story of exits by takeover

EY counted just two UK IPOs in Q1 2026, raising £8.8m and £4m. It blamed a sell-off in sectors seen as exposed to AI disruption, plus the Middle East conflict ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_uk/newsroom/2026/04/uk-ipo-market-saw-cautious-start-to-2026-amid-volatility)). H1 ended with **seven IPOs raising £577m**, and EY expects a rebound in late 2026 or early 2027 ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_uk/newsroom/2026/07/uk-ipo-market-recovery-2026)). Companies are leaving through two routes. **Wise** moved its primary listing to the US and debuted on Nasdaq on 11 May 2026, citing "the world's deepest and most liquid capital market" ([Wise](https://owners.wise.com/news-releases/news-release-details/wise-debuts-us-listing-nasdaq)). Ashtead plans to follow Flutter and CRH ([Yahoo Finance UK](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/london-ipos-dry-2026-firms-230100136.html)). Others are being taken over: **Schroders delisted on 1 October 2026** when Nuveen's takeover completed ([Funds Europe](https://funds-europe.com/schroders-agrees-9-9bn-sale-to-nuveen-ending-200-years-of-family-control/)). Hg-backed Visma provisionally chose London over Amsterdam, then delayed to 2027 after an AI-related tech sell-off ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-27/hg-backed-visma-said-to-delay-planned-london-ipo-to-next-year)). The policy response has been extensive. New UK Listing Rules from July 2024 merged the premium and standard segments, new public-offer rules took effect in January 2026, and AIM and IPO-research reforms followed in summer 2026 ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/uk-capital-markets-reform-where-are-we-now)). Companies listing after November 2025 get a three-year stamp-duty (SDRT) exemption ([Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/uk-capital-markets-reform-where-are-we-now/)). PISCES, a platform for trading private company shares, is now live, although its first trade is variously dated February, Q1 or May 2026 ([WFW](https://www.wfw.com/articles/developments-in-the-london-equity-capital-markets-pisces-and-share-digitisation/)). Critics such as Stifel argue the reforms are not enough while UK pension funds hold so little domestic equity. The figure itself is **contested**, at 1.6% or 4.4% ([Yahoo/Stifel](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/london-capital-markets-reform-needed-131900623.html)). Have a view on both sides. Lazard's UK head of investment banking argued that companies worth "three, four, or five billion would not get the attention of investors in the US" ([Yahoo Finance UK](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/london-ipos-dry-2026-firms-230100136.html)). Anglo Teck keeps its primary listing in London even as its headquarters moves to Vancouver ([Anglo American](https://www.angloamerican.com/media/press-releases/2025/09-12-2025a)).

### Rates have turned, and leveraged buyouts feel it first

The Bank of England cut Bank Rate from 5.25% in August 2024 to **3.75% in December 2025** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/bank-of-england-cuts-key-interest-rate-to-3-75-amid-easing-inflation-125121801027_1.html)) and has held it there through 2026. At the September 2026 meeting it voted **6–3 to hold, with Pill, Mann and Greene voting to hike to 4%**. CPI was **3.1% in August**, and the Bank expects it to rise above 4% in early 2027 as energy prices linked to the Middle East conflict feed through. Governor Bailey warned that the longer energy volatility persists, "the more likely it is we will need to raise Bank Rate" ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026); [MTA](https://www.mta.org.uk/resources/monetary-policy-committee-interest-rate-decision-september-2026/)). The next decisions are on **5 November and 17 December 2026**, and some banks now expect a hike ([XTB](https://www.xtb.com/en/education/uk-interest-rate-forecast)). Check the 5 November outcome before any interview after that date. The ECB raised rates in June and September 2026. PitchBook calls those hikes the clearest driver of an **11% quarter-on-quarter drop in European PE deal value in Q3 2026**, with entry multiples falling to **8.9x EV/EBITDA from 9.8x** in 2025 and US sponsors taking a record 45.7% of deal value ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/us-investors-prop-up-european-pe-as-dealmaking-drops-off-in-q3)). Leveraged finance tells the same story. Q1 2026 European issuance fell 18.9% to €130bn, with high yield down 25.3% and leveraged loans down 27.1%, while **direct lending rose 39.6%** as private credit took share ([AFME](https://www.afme.eu/publications/data-research/european-high-yield-leveraged-loan-and-private-credit-report-q1-2026/)). PE's exit backlog sits at a decade high ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/european-pes-exit-backlog-hits-decade-high)).

The mechanism is what interviewers want to hear. Higher base rates raise debt costs. That means less leverage or lower entry prices for the same IRR, which fits the lower entry multiples. Rate uncertainty also widens the gap between what buyers bid and what sellers ask, which slows exits. Strategic buyers paying in shares, such as McCormick and Anglo American, depend less on leverage. That is why large strategic deals came back while sponsor-led mid-market activity stayed thinner. At group level, M&A coverage (especially FIG and consumer) had a strong 2026, levfin and ECM were softer, and restructuring could pick up if rates rise. That last point is an inference, not a forecast from the sources.

"Why go into banking if AI will do the analyst's job?" is now a fair interview question. The strong answer is that verification, judgement and client trust become the job.

### Build the habit, then structure every answer around "so what"

Use a daily routine of one macro update, one deal or corporate story and one sector follow-up, each written up in two or three lines. About two weeks of this builds a reusable set of talking points ([Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/commercial-awareness-markets-guide)). Read the FT's front section, Companies & Markets and Lex, and use Finimize-style explainers for unfamiliar concepts ([93% Club](https://www.93percent.club/blog/commercial-awareness-students)). "Little and often" beats reading the paper cover to cover ([Legal Cheek](https://www.legalcheek.com/lc-careers-posts/commercial-awareness-why-its-more-than-buzzwords-or-reading-the-ft-cover-to-cover/)). Structure every answer as what happened, why it matters, and the second-order effect on the bank's clients or your division. Aim for **45–60 seconds on HireVue and about 90 seconds live** ([Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/commercial-awareness-markets-guide)). Expect follow-ups such as why a strategic buyer can outbid a PE fund, or how higher rates change acquisition financing ([Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/investment-banking-interview-preparation-guide)). A top answer adds the bank-level layer. For example: "Rates on hold but inflation rising again means a higher-for-longer cost of debt, which squeezes LBO returns and favours cash-rich strategic buyers. That fits large strategic deals returning while sponsor activity fell in Q3." Prepare one deal in depth (ideally one your target bank advised on), one UK macro theme and one view on the listings debate. Write each as a 150-word note covering the catalyst, three rationales, risks and the implication for the division. Using AI to write commercial-awareness answers "will get you exposed" in interview ([FO Perspectives](https://foperspectives.substack.com/p/how-to-use-commercial-awareness-in)).

## Standing out means proof that survives ten minutes of probing

Getting through the funnel and standing out are different problems. In 2026 the second one is mostly about authenticity, depth and evidence.

### London networking buys information and access, not shortcuts

London entry-level recruiting is far more HR-run than in the US. Coffee chats and cold outreach "generally have nowhere near the same return in Europe as in the US", and alumni networks are strong at targets and weak elsewhere ([Wall Street Mastermind](https://wallstmastermind.com/investment-banking-in-london-everything-you-need-to-know/)). Forum users describe London as "largely HR driven" and call cold emailing "useless for banks with an HR division", but say people are "generally pretty happy to help" when there is an introduction or a shared university ([WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/importance-of-networking-in-the-uk); [WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/networking-to-break-into-ib-in-london)). The value of referrals is **contested**. US-oriented guides call them "almost imperative". PrepLounge says a referral mainly gets your application a human review. Baird's European programme states that referrals do not influence selection. London forum users say referrals carry more weight at mid-market banks and some boutiques ([PrepLounge](https://www.preplounge.com/en/blog/finance/investment-banking/interview/referral-in-ib); [Baird](https://www.bairdcareers.com/internships/global-investment-banking-intern-programme-europe/); [WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/job-search/internal-referrals-in-london)).

In London, networking mostly buys you specific material for "why this bank and division", invitations to insight evenings and closed events, early warning of openings, and occasionally a human read at a boutique. In order of return, the best channels are bank-run campus events with a specific follow-up within 24–48 hours, then alumni found through LinkedIn's alumni filter, then people you meet at spring week. Pure cold outreach comes last. Keep messages under about 150 words, open with the shared connection, and make a small, specific request such as a 15–20-minute call. Guides cite 10–30% response rates for this kind of outreach ([PrepLounge](https://www.preplounge.com/en/blog/finance/investment-banking/interview/cold-email-ib); [ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/networking-email-templates-investment-banking)). One recruiter received the **same AI-written "cutting your teeth in finance" email from 15 students** ([Prospect Rock Partners](https://prospectrockpartners.com/15-students-just-sent-me-the-exact-same-cutting-your-teeth-in-finance-cold-email-lets-talk-about-ai-and-networking/)). An unedited AI draft is therefore worse than no message. On a call, ask one or two questions only that person can answer, never ask for a referral directly, and send a same-day thank-you that names one thing you learned.

### AI sameness has made specificity the scarce asset

Freshminds' review of autumn 2025 London internship recruiting, covering more than 8,000 applicants, found CVs "edited and tweaked to be almost perfectly the same". Strong-looking candidates often scored poorly at interview ([Freshminds](https://www.freshminds.co.uk/blog/2026/05/event-review-investment-banking-and-the-application-arms-race)). Recruiters facing AI-polished CVs now "spend longer probing candidates with more specific and technical questions" and focus more on character ([Crain's New York](https://prod.crainsnewyork.com/banking-finance/hiring-junior-bankers-battle-against-ai-written-resumes)). This is the central strategic fact of the 2026/27 cycle. Polish is cheap, and what remains scarce is depth that holds up when someone keeps asking "why?". One model of a real UK company that you built yourself, one written stock pitch and one deal write-up you can defend for ten minutes are worth more than a dozen shallow CV lines.

For AI, keep a short, honest log of specific uses and their limits. Examples: summarising a filing and then checking the figures by hand, speeding up a comps pull, or writing a small Python script. That answers a UBS-style question without raising the "AI wrote my answers" red flag.

### Red flags that sink otherwise strong candidates

The most common red flag is generic motivation ("smart people", "fast-paced"), which invites the follow-up "why not S&T or corporate finance?" ([FE Training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/investment-banking/how-to-answer-the-why-investment-banking-interview-question/)). The most damaging is sounding as if you are already planning your exit, which a former Blackstone MD calls the "WORST possible answer" ([Wall Street Mastermind](https://wallstmastermind.com/why-investment-banking-how-to-answer-this-critical-interview-question/)). Close behind come a vague division preference and, at Goldman, not knowing its 14 Business Principles ([IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/goldman-sachs-interview)), along with exaggerated responsibilities and rambling under stress ([Ask Ivy](http://www.askivy.net/articles/investment-banking/interview-preparation/common-mistakes-in-investment-banking-interviews)). The 2026 addition is AI-written content that falls apart under follow-up questions. At internships, older but still relevant eFinancialCareers advice adds heavy drinking at events, networking into another team and then turning down the original team's offer, and security lapses. A former Goldman partner's advice was to listen more than you talk in the early weeks ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2017/06/15-ways-to-screw-up-your-banking-internship)).

## Interview preparation: motivation, stories, markets and mechanics

UK interviews differ from US ones in their reliance on competency questions and online tests ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking/recruitment/)). Prepare in four layers.

### Each stage weights the four question types differently

Reported spring-week questions are almost entirely motivational and competency-based. Goldman's are "behavioural and competency-based, not technical" ([OurGen](https://ourgen.uk/insights/goldman-sachs-spring-week)). J.P. Morgan asks "Why J.P. Morgan?", which company you follow and which stock interests you ([Glassdoor UK](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/J-P-Morgan-Spring-Week-Insight-Interview-Questions-EI_IE145.0,10_KO11,30.htm)). Summer interviews keep that core and add basic technicals. Some sources claim London assessment centres "frequently skip technical questions altogether", which others **contest** ([SuperdayReady](https://www.superdayready.com/blog/investment-banking-recruiting-by-region)), so prepare for both. The weights below are an **inference from candidate reports**, not sourced percentages.

| Stage | Motivation / CV | Competency | Commercial | Technical |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spring week | ~60% | ~30% | ~10% | ~0% |
| Summer internship (IBD) | ~35% | ~30% | ~15% | ~20% |
| Off-cycle / full-time | Lower | Similar | Higher, plus deep discussion of past deals | Highest |

### Motivation answers: spark, test, fit, firm

Keep "why IB" to about 30–60 seconds. Start from a personal experience "specific to you", and never mention pay ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/why-investment-banking/)). Name the actual work (models, pitchbooks, live transactions), show you have weighed the trade-offs of 80–100-hour weeks, and answer "why not consulting, S&T or corporate finance" before you are asked ([Richmond Career Services](https://careerservices.richmond.edu/blog/2026/02/10/how-to-answer-why-investment-banking-interview-question-2026-guide/)). A four-beat structure synthesised from these sources works well. Open with the **spark**, the moment your interest began, such as a deal you followed, a society pitch or a family business sale. Move to the **test**, what you did to check the interest: a spring week, an insight evening, a model you built, or conversations with analysts, naming what they told you. Then the **fit**, why transaction work suits your strengths better than the alternatives. Close on the **firm**: two specifics about the bank, such as a deal it advised on, the strength of a London sector team or something an employee said, plus one link to your own values. Networking pays off most here, because it turns "why this bank" from adjectives into facts. For a sector interest, pair one recent UK deal with one structural driver. "I'm interested in FIG because wealth-management consolidation drove an eightfold rise in UK FS deal value in H1 2026, as with Evelyn and Aegon" is a credible view. "I like financial institutions" is not. International candidates at J.P. Morgan are advised to show commitment to a long-term UK career ([AccessECS](https://www.accessecs.com/guides/interview-preparation-jpmorgan-international)).

### Competency answers: a ten-story bank built in STAR

Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result), keep answers brief, and prepare **at least two stories per competency** so you can handle variations ([Leland](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/mastering-behavioral-questions-in-investment-banking-interviews-tips-and-tricks)). Interviewers probe decision-making, leadership, teamwork, failure and ethics. A failure story should be real but not fatal, and you should expect follow-ups on its cause and the lesson ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-fit-questions/)). Build eight to ten flexible stories and tag each with the competencies it can cover. A complete bank includes leading a project, teamwork under a deadline, conflict with a teammate, a setback and what changed afterwards, a piece of detailed and accurate work (ideally one where you caught an error, since that is what analysts do all day), persuading someone, an integrity call, adapting to sudden change, something you started on your own initiative, and an analytical project such as a pitch or model. Quantify results, and make sure each story survives two rounds of "why?" or "what would you do differently?". Rehearse on camera to 90–110 seconds so it fits a two-minute HireVue limit.

### Technical questions London interviewers have actually asked

Candidate reports from London summer processes show a consistent core. Glassdoor entries are often hard to date, so treat these as recurring themes rather than a 2026 syllabus.

| Bank | Reported question | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Barclays | "What are the 3 main valuation methodologies?" | [Glassdoor UK](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/Barclays-Investment-Banking-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE3456.0,8_KO9,42.htm) |
| Deutsche Bank | "Walk me through a DCF" | [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Deutsche-Bank-Investment-Banking-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE3150.0,13_KO14,47.htm) |
| Rothschild & Co | DCF walk-through; "Which valuation methodology produces the highest value?" | [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Rothschild-and-Co-Summer-Investment-Banking-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE9944.0,17_KO18,51.htm) |
| Goldman Sachs | "How do rising tax rates affect a DCF?"; a recent M&A deal you have followed | [Glassdoor UK](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/Goldman-Sachs-Investment-Banking-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE2800.0,13_KO14,47.htm) |
| Lazard | "What is PIK interest?" | [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Lazard-Investment-Banking-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE5784.0,6_KO7,40.htm) |
| UBS (guide compilation, not verified candidate reports) | Terminal value as g approaches WACC; deferred tax assets and liabilities | [Aced](https://www.aced.io/guides/ubs-investment-banking-summer-analyst-interview) |

Each is answered directly by the lessons above. On the tax question, a higher tax rate cuts NOPAT and therefore UFCF. It also lowers the after-tax cost of debt, which nudges WACC down slightly. For most companies the cash-flow effect dominates, so value falls (derived from the UFCF and WACC formulas). As g approaches WACC, the Gordon denominator approaches zero and terminal value explodes, which is why g must stay below WACC and roughly in line with long-run nominal GDP. PIK interest accrues to the loan principal instead of being paid in cash, which preserves cash flow at the cost of a growing debt balance. It is a standard feature of LBO tests ([Wall Street Prep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/leveraged-buyout-lbo-modeling-1-hour-practice-test)). The repeated "highest method" and "tax effect" questions show that London interviewers test whether you understand the mechanics, not whether you have memorised definitions.

### Running the HireVue, the group exercise and the superday

On HireVue, answer within the first sentence, stay specific, and rehearse on camera because many platforms allow one attempt. In group exercises, assessors reward listening, building on others' points, keeping time aloud and reaching a defensible group answer. Dominating is penalised, so the best role is often the person who summarises where the group stands. In individual case presentations of the Rothschild or Lazard type, lead with a clear recommendation, give two or three supporting reasons drawn from numbers in the pack, name the key risks, and finish on time. Goldman superdays are described as three to five back-to-back interviews, usually in person ([Ophy AI](https://ophyai.com/blog/company-guides/goldman-sachs-interview-guide)). Prepare a clean 60–90-second CV walk-through, and treat each interviewer as a fresh audience.

## Spring weeks and internships are auditions you can win

### At spring week, banks assess potential and engagement, not technique

Spring-week interns produce no real work, so banks judge engagement, intelligent questions, reliability, cultural fit and evidence that you learned during the week. You are observed in presentations, at networking events and in casual conversations with analysts ([AccessECS](https://www.accessecs.com/blog/investment-banking-spring-week-2026-complete-guide); [M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/)). There is usually, though not always, an assignment or end-of-week interview that decides the summer offer or fast track ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.fi/news/spring-week-bank)). Evercore's London spring programme, for example, includes a mock M&A transaction alongside CV and interview workshops ([Evercore](https://www.evercore.com/careers/students-graduates/students-graduates-europe-asia/)). A routine built from these sources starts before the week: research every team and speaker, and prepare two or three sharp questions per session tied to a recent deal. During the week, meet as many analysts and associates as you can and note one personal detail per person the same evening. In the group task, structure and summarise rather than dominate, and treat the final assignment like a client deliverable, with no errors. Within 48 hours of the end, send each person you met a personalised thank-you, then follow up later with something concrete, such as "I built a model of X after our conversation".

### At a summer internship, the analysts' roundtable decides

The ten-week summer internship is effectively "a 10-week interview" ([LearnSignal](https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/how-to-become-an-investment-banker-uk/)). The analysts and associates who work most closely with interns "have significant clout" on offers. Win at least one strong advocate, ideally several, and never go around juniors to court senior bankers ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-internship-guide/)). Offers are typically decided at an end-of-programme "roundtable" where analysts compare notes. The criteria are "attention to detail above all", reliability and communication ([ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/how-to-get-return-offer-summer-internship)). At Lazard, interns give a 10-minute presentation to senior bankers in the final week, which together with their internship performance decides the offer ([Lazard](https://www.lazard.com/about-lazard/locations/united-kingdom/careers-in-the-united-kingdom/early-career-programmes-in-the-uk/)). The working habits follow directly. Triple-check every number, format and source. Confirm instructions back in writing, give status updates before you are asked, and own mistakes immediately. Build relationships with analysts first, because they speak first at the roundtable.

### When conversion fails, the summer route is still open

A spring week that does not convert does not end your candidacy. You can still apply through the normal summer route, and off-cycle roles offer another path ([AccessECS](https://www.accessecs.com/blog/investment-banking-spring-week-2026-complete-guide)). M&I is explicit that spring weeks help but are not required, and London internship offers are won without them ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/)). Ask for feedback, turn what you learned into specifics for the next "why this bank" answer, and apply early in August–September of the following year.

### Penultimate-year students: salvage summer 2027 and widen the net

If you are in the penultimate year of a three-year degree, the summer 2027 cycle is late but not over. Apply this week to whatever is still open: Goldman Sachs (rolling), PJT, TD Securities (by 25 October), Peel Hunt (by 4 December) and the live Barclays listing ([Intervyo](https://www.intervyo.co.uk/tracker); [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/careers/students/programs-and-internships/emea/summer-analyst-programme)). In parallel, target mid-market and boutique firms as they post. London off-cycle roles are "formal, advertised, well-established programmes, not favours" ([ibinterviewquestions](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/investment-banking-london-uk-guide)), and continental off-cycle roles open three to six months before their start dates. Your interviews will be summer-level, so compress technical Lessons 1–7 into the next six to eight weeks. Have one deal and one market view ready, because summer HireVues mix behavioural and technical questions ([Glassdoor UK](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/Goldman-Sachs-Investment-Banking-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE2800.0,13_KO14,47.htm)). If summer 2027 fails, the final-year fallback is direct graduate hiring, though such roles are scarce and some ask for prior IB experience. TD Securities' 2027 graduate analyst role requires a 2:1 and prior corporate finance or IB experience, and Deutsche Bank allows only one graduate application per UK season ([The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/jobs/tdbank/2027-graduate-programme-investment-banking-analyst)). Use off-cycle internships that lead into graduate roles, such as BofA's, which requires availability from July 2027.

### Four-year courses, international students and non-target universities

Second-years on four-year courses are usually still eligible for spring weeks this cycle, and if your course includes a placement year, the industrial placement window opens in September of second year, earlier than most expect. International students should note that spring weeks and summer internships fall in vacations and therefore fit Student-visa rules. Graduates applying from 1 January 2027 get 18 months on the Graduate Route rather than 24, so a sponsored analyst role matters more as a destination. The High Potential Individual visa list roughly doubled to about 80 universities from November 2025 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/high-potential-individual-visa-global-universities-list/high-potential-individual-visa-global-universities-list-2025)). Non-target students should apply early to spring weeks and access programmes, use mid-market banks and UK brokers as stepping stones, and treat a second language as a passport to continental off-cycle seats.

### The backup ladder if the first cycle fails

Big 4 lead advisory is a credible route, not a consolation. KPMG and PwC tied third by European M&A deal count in H1 2026, and KPMG ranked first in Q1 ([Private Banker International](https://www.privatebankerinternational.com/news/europe-ma-advisers-h1-2026/); [Private Banker International](https://www.privatebankerinternational.com/news/europe-ma-advisers-q1-2026/)). Forum users describe a Big 4 to mid-market bank move as "definitely achievable, especially if you stick it out for 3 years and get your ACA", with a later move sideways into a bulge bracket or elite boutique ([WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/re-big-4-to-ibd-how-does-it-happen-in-the-uk)). Prefer lead advisory or corporate finance over transaction services, which leans towards due diligence. A pre-experience master's acts as a reset. IB took 33% of financial-services hires from LBS's 2022 Masters in Finance class (historical) and 21% of Cambridge's 2024/25 MFin cohort ([LBS](https://assets.london.edu/hxo16fanegqh/c7089a37-a80b-4175-b344-1914ad1ea176/352ee6ab0277e1a9d2de3a47ab0a7c1f/mif_employment_report.pdf); [Cambridge Judge](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2026/the-master-of-finance-employment-report-2026/)). Because UBS now asks 2027 graduates for demonstrable AI skills, anyone re-entering through a master's should build that evidence too.

## Conclusion

Most of the advantage in London banking recruiting comes from timing, which is unusual among competitive careers. Rolling review, one application per firm and spring-week fast tracks mean the decisive work for an analyst job starting in 2029 happens in October and November 2026, before most first-years have sat a university exam. Two forces are changing what that work rewards. Generative AI has made written applications cheap and identical, so banks have moved their judgement to tests, video and in-person rounds, and recruiters probe harder once you are in the room. And as AI takes over first drafts, the junior job is shifting from producing analysis to verifying it. Both forces reward the same profile: a candidate who knows the mechanics well enough to catch a wrong number, writes and speaks in their own voice, and can show proof of work. Memorised answers and polished prose matter less than they did.

The 2026 context helps the prepared candidate in specific ways. IFRS 18 takes effect on 1 January 2027, inside this recruiting cycle, so the cash-flow reclassification is a live technical talking point that most US-centric prep misses. The UK market story connects rates, LBO returns, foreign bids for UK plcs and the listings debate, and a student who can trace that chain sounds like an analyst. Spring-week direct conversion fell from 31% to 20% in one year, and offers are concentrating among the best-prepared tenth of applicants. The sensible strategy is fewer, earlier and deeper applications backed by verifiable work, with mid-market, off-cycle, continental and Big 4 routes planned as parallel options rather than consolation prizes.
