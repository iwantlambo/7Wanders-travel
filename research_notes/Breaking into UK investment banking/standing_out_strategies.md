## 1. Networking in the UK context (vs US-style; LinkedIn, alumni, societies, events, follow-up, informational calls, referrals)

### Takeaway
London entry-level recruiting is far more HR-run and application-driven than the US, so cold emailing and coffee chats rarely "push" a CV through by themselves. Networking still pays off for a top candidate, but mainly in other ways: getting into firm-run and invite-only events, collecting specific details for motivation answers, and occasionally getting a human to review the application (mostly at boutiques and mid-market firms). Alumni and warm introductions get far better response rates than cold messages, and generic or AI-written outreach is now easy to spot.

### Cited Findings
**How London differs from the US**
- A UK guide says the UK market "historically relied on structured graduate scheme applications". It also says LinkedIn outreach and spring weeks are becoming more important in London. In the US, by contrast, cold emailing alumni and booking 20-minute informational calls is described as near-mandatory before a CV screen — [Intervyo](https://www.intervyo.co.uk/advice/networking-and-coffee-chats)
- Wall Street Mastermind says US tactics can fail in Europe and that coffee chats and cold outreach "generally have nowhere near the same return in Europe as in the US". It ties this to school tier: alumni networks are "fairly strong" at target universities and weak at non-targets — [Wall Street Mastermind, IB in London](https://wallstmastermind.com/investment-banking-in-london-everything-you-need-to-know/)
- WSO forum users say London entry-level recruitment is "largely HR driven", whereas US bankers have "far more influence". One user calls cold emailing "useless for banks with an HR division". Another says "networking is always a way in", with coffee meetings following cold outreach. Forum evidence is anecdotal and conflicting — [WSO: Importance of networking in the UK](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/importance-of-networking-in-the-uk); [WSO: Are coffee chats/LinkedIn a thing in London?](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/are-coffee-chats-and-reaching-out-by-linkedin-a-thing-in-london); [WSO: How important is networking in the UK](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/job-search/how-important-is-networking-in-the-uk-london)
- On WSO, people are "generally pretty happy to help" when there is an introduction or a shared university. A pure cold email from a non-target student is likely to go unanswered. One user says the best way to network in London is through "invite-only firm-specific events" — [WSO: Networking to break into IB in London](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/networking-to-break-into-ib-in-london); [WSO: Networking in London](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/job-search/networking-in-london-3)
- Mergers & Inquisitions' networking guide has a section on the belief that "networking doesn't work in London". Its answer was not visible in the search snippet — [M&I, IB Networking](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-networking/)

**Cold email / LinkedIn mechanics (mostly US-derived guidance)**
- Keep cold emails under about 150 words and personalise each one. Contact alumni first, then people from your hometown or past employers, then bankers in your target group. Alumni links give the best response rates — [PrepLounge, IB cold email](https://www.preplounge.com/en/blog/finance/investment-banking/interview/cold-email-ib)
- One template guide puts response rates for cold outreach at 10-30%. It suggests 5-10 personalised emails a week during recruiting season, sent Tuesday to Thursday. Its structure: a subject line naming your university and purpose, an opener built on a shared connection, then a specific, low-commitment ask such as 15-20 minutes on the phone — [IB Interview Questions, networking templates](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/networking-email-templates-investment-banking)
- A finance recruiter received the same AI-generated "cutting your teeth in finance" cold email from 15 students. Generic, overly formal, interchangeable messages are now easy to spot — [Prospect Rock Partners](https://prospectrockpartners.com/15-students-just-sent-me-the-exact-same-cutting-your-teeth-in-finance-cold-email-lets-talk-about-ai-and-networking/); [Adventis, using AI in IB recruiting](https://www.adventiscg.com/blog-articles/how-to-and-how-not-to-use-ai-for-investment-banking-recruiting)

**Referrals at London banks (contested)**
- Street of Walls (US-oriented) says it is "almost imperative" to have internal referrals because HR uses them to thin the first-round pile — [Street of Walls](https://www.streetofwalls.com/finance-training-courses/investment-banking-overview-and-behavioral-training/investment-banking-networking/)
- PrepLounge says a referral mainly gets your application a human review and sometimes a first-round interview, and after that you are judged on skills. It adds that bankers rarely refer someone they haven't vetted — [PrepLounge, referrals in IB](https://www.preplounge.com/en/blog/finance/investment-banking/interview/referral-in-ib)
- Baird's London/Europe intern programme FAQ says referrals only help it understand how candidates heard about the programme and do not influence selection — [Baird Europe IB internship](https://www.bairdcareers.com/internships/global-investment-banking-intern-programme-europe/)
- WSO London users say referral networks exist but "don't have much influence" on outcomes, with exceptions. The approach is said to work better at mid-market banks and some elite boutiques — [WSO: Internal referrals in London](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/job-search/internal-referrals-in-london)
- London off-cycle internships are "formal, advertised, well-established programmes, not favours" — [IB Interview Questions, London guide](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/investment-banking-london-uk-guide)

**Diversity/access programmes**
- SEO London runs an Investment Banking & Asset Management programme, a structured access route for under-represented students (only its existence was confirmed, not outcome data) — [SEO London](https://www.seo-london.org/ibandam)

### Inferences
- In London, what networking buys you is mostly information and access, not CV-pushing: better "why this bank/division" answers, invitations to insight evenings and closed events, early warning of deadlines, and sometimes a referral at a boutique or mid-market firm. A top candidate networks to sharpen applications rather than to skip them.
- Best return on effort, by inference from the sources: (1) bank-run campus presentations and insight evenings, where you meet bankers face to face and send a specific follow-up within 24-48 hours; (2) alumni from your university and society, found through LinkedIn's alumni filter; (3) people you met at spring week. Pure cold outreach to strangers is the lowest-yield channel.
- A London-appropriate LinkedIn note, built only from the cited guidance (under 150 words, shared connection, specific small ask): "Hi [Name], I'm a first-year [course] student at [Uni] and saw you were also at [Uni/society]. I've been following [specific deal or sector the person worked on, e.g. from the bank's press release] and I'm applying for [bank]'s spring week in [division]. Would you have 15 minutes in the next few weeks for a quick call about how you found the [division] team? Totally understand if you're busy." Never paste an AI draft unedited, because identical AI messages are being recognised.
- A good 15-minute informational call (inferred): one or two questions only this person can answer (their path, what their team actually does, a recent deal they can speak about publicly), no "can you refer me", and a thank-you the same day that names one specific thing you learned. If the call goes well, ask whether there is anyone else they'd suggest you speak to.

### Gaps
- No primary data (bank or Financial News) on how much London banks weight referrals or networking in 2025-26. fnlondon.com could not be searched.
- No sourced London-specific follow-up cadence (e.g. when to send a second nudge). M&I's London networking section could not be read.
- No sourced evidence on whether LinkedIn "connect with note" works better than email in London.
- No bank-specific data on whether insight-evening attendance is tracked or affects screening.

## 2. Commercial awareness (definition, how tested, how to build, deal discussion, markets talking points)

### Takeaway
UK employers test commercial awareness as applied reasoning, not news recall. Strong candidates can say what happened, why it matters, and what it means for the bank's clients or for the division they're applying to, in a tight 60-90 seconds. Habit beats cramming: a short daily routine, the FT (especially Lex), and one or two stories you know in real depth. Late-2026 UK talking points include: Bank Rate on hold at 3.75% with inflation rising again; a very quiet IPO market despite big listing-rule reforms; and UK M&A in financial services and wealth management rising sharply in value.

### Cited Findings
**What it is and how it's tested**
- Bright Network: firms test your knowledge of the sector and the company's environment. The strongest candidates understand how business changes affect the firm — [Bright Network, What is commercial awareness?](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/graduate-career-advice/what-is-commercial-awareness/)
- One definition: knowing how a company makes money, who its competitors are, what challenges it faces, and how political, economic and tech trends might affect it. Finance-specific guidance calls it "applied reasoning rather than commentary" — [93% Club](https://www.93percent.club/blog/commercial-awareness-students); [Finbound, commercial awareness](https://www.finbound.org/blog/commercial-awareness-markets-guide)
- An international-students guide to J.P. Morgan says the firm expects candidates to carry "a live view on recent deals, sector dynamics, and regulatory direction" — [AccessECS, J.P. Morgan interview prep](https://www.accessecs.com/guides/interview-preparation-jpmorgan-international)
- Goldman Sachs' guidance to applicants encourages studying the firm's financial results, business principles and core values — [eFinancialCareers, Goldman HireVue](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/goldman-hirevue)
- Expect "tell me about a recent deal" and a commercial question early in the round. Follow-ups may test why a strategic buyer can pay more than a PE fund, or how higher rates affect acquisition financing — [Finbound, IB interview prep](https://www.finbound.org/blog/investment-banking-interview-preparation-guide); [Intern Game Plan, commercial awareness](https://interngameplan.com/commercial-awareness/)

**How to build it**
- Daily routine: one macro update, one corporate or deal story and one sector follow-up, each written up briefly (headline, interpretation, application, or "what changed / why it matters / where it could come up"). About two weeks of this builds a reusable set of talking points — [Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/commercial-awareness-markets-guide)
- Reading list: the FT front section, Companies & Markets pages and the Lex column; Bloomberg for markets; Finimize for explanations of concepts. Some firms run their own podcasts, which make good interview material — [93% Club](https://www.93percent.club/blog/commercial-awareness-students)
- You don't need to read the FT cover to cover. Build the habit "little and often" — [Legal Cheek](https://www.legalcheek.com/lc-careers-posts/commercial-awareness-why-its-more-than-buzzwords-or-reading-the-ft-cover-to-cover/)
- Go deep on one story and compare how different outlets cover it — [Commercial Law Academy](https://www.commerciallaw.academy/pages/blog?p=the-myth-of-commercial-awareness)
- Warwick's finance societies publish their own commercial-awareness guidance, an example of societies acting as a resource — [Warwick Finance Societies](https://www.warwickfinancesocieties.org/copy-of-cl-advice)
- Caveat: candidates who spend all their prep reading the FT and skip competency practice can lose out. Using AI to write commercial-awareness answers "will get you exposed" in interview — [Commercial Law Academy](https://www.commerciallaw.academy/pages/blog?p=the-myth-of-commercial-awareness); [FO Perspectives](https://foperspectives.substack.com/p/how-to-use-commercial-awareness-in)

**Deal or market discussion structure**
- Four-step answer starting with "what happened", stated as the catalyst in one sentence. Aim for 45-60 seconds on HireVue and about 90 seconds in live rounds. Tie each implication to your division: M&A cares about timing and valuation, Markets about positioning — [Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/commercial-awareness-markets-guide)
- Deal answer: a deal summary, three key rationales, and why it interests you. Pull the press release and outside commentary (e.g. FT) — [Intern Game Plan](https://interngameplan.com/commercial-awareness/) (attribution from search summary; may be a different guide in the same result set)
- Structure: what happened, why it matters, then the second-order effect on a sector or deal type. Practise one behavioural answer, one technical sequence and one commercial discussion aloud — [Finbound, interview prep](https://www.finbound.org/blog/investment-banking-interview-preparation-guide)

**UK market talking points as of autumn 2026 (verify before use)**
- *Bank of England:* Bank Rate held at 3.75% on 17 Sept 2026 by a 6-3 vote, with three members voting to hike to 4.00%. CPI was 3.1% y/y in August; about 0.7pp of the 1.1pp overshoot was from energy, mainly motor fuels. Services inflation was 3.4%, down from 4.5% in March. The MPC expects CPI of about 3.75% in Q4 2026 and slightly above 4% in Q1 2027, and warned policy may have to tighten if the Middle East conflict persists — [TrendForce](https://datatrack.trendforce.com/blog/content/64336/bank-of-england-holds-rates-for-sixth-consecutive-time-q3-benchmark-interest-rate-maintained-at-375-inflation-rebound-sparks-rate-hike-concerns); [MTS Insights](https://www.mtsinsights.com/summaries/10990/); [OrbitRemit](https://blog.orbitremit.com/bank-of-england-september-2026/). - *IPO market:* EY counted only two UK IPOs in Q1 2026 (£8.8m on the Main Market, £4m on AIM). EY's Scott McCubbin blamed a sell-off in sectors seen as exposed to AI disruption, plus the Middle East conflict. Lazard's UK head of investment banking argued that companies worth "three, four, or five billion would not get the attention of investors in the US". Visma's London float was reportedly at risk of delay. Waterstones and Gymshark have held IPO talks — [Yahoo Finance UK](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/london-ipos-dry-2026-firms-230100136.html); [Legal Cheek](https://www.legalcheek.com/lc-journal-posts/from-gymshark-to-monzo-uk-ipos-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- What sets a top answer apart is the "so what for the bank" layer. Example: "Rates on hold but inflation rising again means a higher-for-longer cost of debt, which hurts LBO maths and leaves corporates with strong balance sheets better placed. That fits the pattern of large strategic deals coming back while the mid-market stays thin."
- Prepare one deal you know in depth, ideally one the target bank advised on (from its press releases or league-table news), plus one UK macro theme and one listings-debate view. Be ready to defend a contrarian angle: for example, whether the 2024 listing reforms are enough, or why a £3-5bn company might stay in London.
- Write each story down as a single summary of about 150 words (catalyst, rationale x3, risks, implication for the division). This doubles as material for a newsletter or blog (see section 3).

## 3. Differentiators with evidence of impact (investment funds, pitches, case competitions, writing, models, work experience, societies, CFA, coding/AI, languages)

### Takeaway
There is little hard data on what moves the needle. The best-supported differentiators in 2025-26 are: (1) relevant experience (any finance or boutique internship, an earlier spring week); (2) concrete proof-of-work that holds up under questioning, such as a stock pitch, a model or a leadership role in a student fund; (3) languages in London; and (4) newly, demonstrable practical AI fluency (UBS now formally requires it). CFA Level I as a student is contested and generally seen as a weak signal. Python helps but doesn't get you in for coverage roles.

### Cited Findings
- **Competition context:** UK graduate competition hit a record 140 applications per vacancy in the ISE's 2025 survey, up from 86 in 2022/23 — [ISE top 10 stats of 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/). A recruiter-written Medium guide claims Goldman received 17,000 applications for 450 spring week places (2.6%). This is single-source, from a commercial seller, and unverified — [Elite Careers Strategy, Medium](https://elitecareersstrategy.medium.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-enter-investment-banking-from-a-recruiter-who-placed-candidates-at-goldman-1d595ed0dd4e)
- **Prior spring week = interview advantage:** a report cited in eFC-related coverage found that only about a quarter of accepted summer interns in financial services had done a spring week, but those who had were "nearly twice as likely" to get a summer interview — [eFinancialCareers, no spring week](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/no-spring-internship-bank) (attribution via search summary)
- **Sameness problem (London, 2025-26):** Freshminds' review of autumn 2025 London internship recruiting (8,000+ applicants) found CVs "edited and tweaked to be almost perfectly the same", and strong-looking candidates often scored poorly in interviews — [Freshminds event review, May 2026](https://www.freshminds.co.uk/blog/2026/05/event-review-investment-banking-and-the-application-arms-race)
- **Student funds, stock pitches:** a London guide lists "demonstrated technical initiative" as something London banks look for, with a student investment fund as an example and a CFA Level I attempt also mentioned — [IB Interview Questions, London guide](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/investment-banking-london-uk-guide). A 2026 CV guide recommends writing a 1-2 page stock pitch (thesis, valuation, risks), treating the fund seriously and aiming for leadership to get concrete CV bullets and interview stories — [City Investment Training, IB CV 2026](https://www.cityinvestmenttraining.com/post/how-to-create-a-investment-banking-cv-in-2026)
- **CFA (contested):** M&I calls "highly recommended" for IB "a stretch" and notes the CFA doesn't give access to recruiters or on-campus recruiting. It also lists the CFA among things bankers "don't like" as a substitute for experience — [M&I, CFA for IB](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/cfa-for-investment-banking/); [M&I, stuff bankers don't like](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/stuff-investment-bankers-dont-like-cfa-activities-advanced-degrees/). The London guide above counts a Level I attempt as a positive signal, so the two disagree.
- **What recruiters weigh most:** a 2:1 (minimum), relevant work experience, and extracurriculars. One source says extracurriculars matter more than technical skill at interview — [IB Interview Questions, London guide](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/investment-banking-london-uk-guide); [M&I, IB in London](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-in-london/)
- **Languages:** described as "a genuine differentiator in London in a way it simply is not in New York". Fluent German, French, Italian or Arabic gives access to desks monolingual candidates can't reach. The same guide puts London analyst classes at about 150-200 vs about 300-350 in New York (unverified) — [IB Interview Questions, London guide](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/investment-banking-london-uk-guide)
- **Python/coding:** "a differentiator, not a gatekeeper". For coverage seats, Excel and modelling are "non-negotiable", and Python signals you can handle messy data faster. Python demand is concentrated in quant, risk, trading and data roles and barely registers in pure IB postings — [Disruption Banking, Aug 2026](https://www.disruptionbanking.com/2026/08/10/is-python-gaining-popularity-with-investment-bankers/). Banks will test you on Python if you list it — [eFinancialCareers, Python interview questions](https://www.efinancialcareers.fr/en/news/python-interview-questions)
- **AI fluency (new, 2026):** UBS (as reported by the FT) requires 2027 graduates and interns in Global Banking and Markets to show AI skills, and interviewers will ask how candidates have used AI tools. The bar is described as practical use for analysis, research and client work, not just consumer chatbot familiarity. The 2:1 requirement is unchanged — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ubs-ai-skills-junior-bankers-2027-graduate-hiring); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/at-ubs-ai-skills-are-now-a-condition-for-landing-a-job/); [Fintech Garden, 7 Sept 2026](https://fintech.garden/news/2026-09-07-ubs-makes-ai-proficiency-a-formal-hiring-requirement-for-junior-bankers-and-inte/). UBS's graduate programme includes an "AI Fluency Pathway" — [UBS Graduate Talent Program](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/careers/early-careers/graduate-talent-program.html). Santander is reportedly recruiting "advanced AI users" into some graduate programmes (secondary report only) — [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/9bb076e3-088c-4751-8b00-f9b605beb6f8)
- **No spring week?** eFC says students without a spring week still have routes, and off-cycle internships are common in EMEA — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/no-spring-internship-bank); [M&I, London accelerated recruiting](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-accelerated-recruiting-london)

### Inferences
- Given the "everyone's CV looks the same" finding, the most valuable differentiator is depth that holds up under probing. One self-built model of a real UK company, one written stock pitch and one deal write-up you can defend for 10 minutes are worth more than several shallow CV lines.
- A finance newsletter or blog (e.g. a weekly 300-word UK deal note on Substack or LinkedIn) is plausible proof of commercial awareness and writing ability. It also feeds directly into interview answers and gives you something to share when networking. No sourced evidence of impact was found, so present it as an inference.
- For AI: keep a short, honest record of specific ways you've used AI tools (e.g. summarising filings and then checking them by hand, speeding up a comps table, building a model in Python), plus the limits you noticed. This addresses UBS-style questions without falling into the "AI wrote my answers" red flag (section 6).
- Case competitions and society leadership: no direct evidence of impact was found, but they generate STAR stories and give you recruiter contact at sponsored events. Treat them as story generators and access points, not as signals in themselves.
- Big 4, start-up or boutique experience: most valuable when you can explain a transferable skill (Excel, client contact, deadlines) and how it led you towards banking.

### Gaps
- No bank-published data on how they weight student funds, case competitions, newsletters or CFA. No quantified evidence of impact for any single differentiator.
- Couldn't confirm the "twice as likely" spring-week statistic against its original report.
- No London-specific recruiter survey on Python or AI skills beyond UBS.

## 4. Motivational answers ("why IB", "why this bank", sector or product interest)

### Takeaway
Generic motivation ("fast-paced", "smart people", "learning curve") is the most common failure. Great answers are short (about 30-60 seconds), anchored in a personal moment, show you understand the actual analyst work, answer "why not consulting, S&T or corporate finance" ahead of the follow-up, and tie the bank's specific strengths (deals, sector teams, culture, business principles) to the experience you want. Never sound like you're planning your exit.

### Cited Findings
- Cite a personal experience "specific to you", keep it to about 30-40 seconds, and don't mention pay — [M&I, Why investment banking](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/why-investment-banking/)
- Common lines like "smart people" and "fast-paced environment" don't set you apart. Referencing the concrete work (Excel models, pitch books, live transactions) signals that you've done your research. Interviewers often follow up with "why not S&T?" or "why not corporate finance?" — [FE Training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/investment-banking/how-to-answer-the-why-investment-banking-interview-question/); [IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/investment-banking-interview-questions)
- Explain why this career over consulting, corporate finance or trading, be specific about M&A advisory or capital raising, and show you've weighed the trade-offs (80-100 hour weeks, little control over your time) — [Richmond Career Services, 2026 guide](https://careerservices.richmond.edu/blog/2026/02/10/how-to-answer-why-investment-banking-interview-question-2026-guide/)
- "Why this firm": add a sentence linking the firm's differentiators to the analyst experience you expect — [Leland](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/why-investment-banking-interview-question)
- A London-focused recruiter guide recommends a chronological, logical, memorable story that starts with how your interest began (commercial source) — [Elite Careers Strategy, Medium](https://elitecareersstrategy.medium.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-enter-investment-banking-from-a-recruiter-who-placed-candidates-at-goldman-1d595ed0dd4e)
- A former Blackstone MD: the "WORST possible answer is making it sound like you're already planning your exit" — [Wall Street Mastermind, Why IB](https://wallstmastermind.com/why-investment-banking-how-to-answer-this-critical-interview-question/) (attribution via search summary)
- Goldman: learn about the specific division you're applying to. A vague division preference can be a red flag. Recruiter calls often cover "Why Goldman Sachs?". Not knowing Goldman's 14 Business Principles is a common mistake — [IGotAnOffer, Goldman interview](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/goldman-sachs-interview); [Ophy AI Goldman guide](https://ophyai.com/blog/company-guides/goldman-sachs-interview-guide)
- Giving no division preference is flagged as a common error in UK spring week applications — [Finbound, spring week guide 2027](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-application-guide)
- For international applicants at J.P. Morgan: show commitment to a long-term UK career — [AccessECS](https://www.accessecs.com/guides/interview-preparation-jpmorgan-international)

### Inferences
- A suggested structure, synthesised from the sources: (1) Spark: a specific moment, e.g. a deal you followed, a society pitch, a family business sale. (2) Test: what you did to check the interest, e.g. a spring week, an insight evening, building a model, conversations with analysts, naming people and what they told you. (3) Fit: why the analyst job (transactions, modelling, client work) suits your strengths better than consulting or S&T. (4) Firm: two things specific to the bank, such as a deal it advised on, the strength of a sector team in London, or something an employee said, plus one link to your own values.
- For a sector or product interest, name one recent UK deal in that sector and one structural driver. Examples: wealth-management consolidation (supported by EY's H1 2026 data in section 2), or ECM and the UK listings reform. This turns "I'm interested in FIG" into a credible view.
- Information gathered through networking (section 1) is what makes "why this bank" specific. This is probably the highest-return use of networking in London.

### Gaps
- No primary London bank recruiter quotes (Rothschild, Lazard, GS London, JPM London) on what makes a great motivation answer. Bank careers "tips" pages could not be fetched or found.
- Most motivation guidance is US-origin. Its fit with UK competency-style interviews is assumed, not evidenced.

## 5. Behavioural/competency interviews (STAR, story bank, common UK questions)

### Takeaway
UK banks run competency-heavy screens (HireVue with roughly 30 seconds to prepare and 2 minutes to answer at Goldman, then live interviews and superdays). Use STAR, keep answers concise, and prepare at least two stories per competency so you can handle variations. Core competencies: teamwork, leadership, failure, conflict/difficult colleague, decision-making, ethics, adaptability, communication/influencing, plus "walk me through your CV".

### Cited Findings
- STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most widely recommended framework. Have at least two examples per competency and keep answers brief, focused on the situation, the action taken and a positive outcome — [Leland, 20 behavioural questions](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/mastering-behavioral-questions-in-investment-banking-interviews-tips-and-tricks); [Intern Game Plan, competency questions](https://interngameplan.com/competency-interview-questions/)
- Interviewers probe decision-making, leadership, teamwork, failure and ethical choices. Common fit prompts include a team activity that didn't go to plan. For failure, choose a story that is real but not fatal, and expect follow-ups on the cause and the lesson — [M&I, fit questions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-fit-questions/); [Leland](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/mastering-behavioral-questions-in-investment-banking-interviews-tips-and-tricks)
- Wider competency lists include adaptability, communication and influencing — [Ask Ivy, competency questions](http://www.askivy.net/articles/investment-banking/interview-preparation/list-of-competency-interview-questions); [WikiJob, 100 IB interview questions](https://www.wikijob.co.uk/industry/investment-banking/investment-banking-interview-questions)
- Goldman's HireVue gives 30 seconds to prepare and 120 seconds to answer. Recording yourself in mock interviews beforehand is advised — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/goldman-hirevue); [Graduates First](https://www.graduatesfirst.com/goldman-sachs-aptitude-tests)
- Goldman superdays are described as 3-5 back-to-back 30-minute interviews, usually in person — [Ophy AI](https://ophyai.com/blog/company-guides/goldman-sachs-interview-guide)
- UK spring week and internship application funnels include application form, psychometric tests, HireVue, then live interviews. Assessment-centre group exercises are scored on collaboration, not dominance — [eFinancialCareers, spring weeks](https://www.efinancialcareers.fi/news/spring-week-bank); [AccessECS, spring week 2026](https://www.accessecs.com/blog/investment-banking-spring-week-2026-complete-guide)
- Exaggerating your responsibilities and talking too much under stress are common mistakes — [Ask Ivy, common mistakes](http://www.askivy.net/articles/investment-banking/interview-preparation/common-mistakes-in-investment-banking-interviews)
- Recruiters responding to AI-polished CVs now "spend longer probing candidates with more specific and technical questions" and focus more on character — [Crain's New York, Feb 2025](https://prod.crainsnewyork.com/banking-finance/hiring-junior-bankers-battle-against-ai-written-resumes)

### Inferences
- An 8-10 story bank covering about 8 competencies, each story flexible enough for two or three questions: (1) leading a team or society project; (2) teamwork under a deadline; (3) conflict with a teammate; (4) failure or setback and what changed afterwards; (5) a high-quality piece of detailed work (catching an error matters in banking); (6) persuading or influencing someone; (7) an ethical dilemma or integrity call; (8) adapting to sudden change; (9) initiative, i.e. something you started; (10) a commercial or analytical project (pitch or model). Tag each story with the competencies it covers.
- Quantify results (money raised, members, rankings, time saved). Make sure each story can survive two "why?" or "what would you do differently?" follow-ups, because interviewers now probe more to cut through AI polish.
- Practise HireVue answers to fit 90-110 seconds so you have room within the 120-second limit.

### Gaps
- No official UK bank list of competency questions. The lists come from prep sites.
- No recruiter-sourced data on which competencies carry the most weight at London banks.

## 6. Common mistakes and red flags (generic answers, arrogance, weak deal knowledge, inflated CVs, AI-written content)

### Takeaway
In 2025-26 the defining red flag is AI-generated sameness: identical CVs, cover letters and cold emails that fall apart once an interviewer probes. Other recurring red flags: generic motivation, no division preference, exaggerated CV claims, rambling, signalling an exit plan, weak knowledge of the firm, and (at internships) bad behaviour at social events or ignoring junior staff. Using AI during an interview counts as misconduct at Goldman and others.

### Cited Findings
- **AI in interviews:** Goldman Sachs' campus recruiting team emailed students (June 2025) that the bank bars outside help during interviews, naming ChatGPT and Google — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/goldman-hirevue); [Fortune, 11 June 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/06/11/goldman-sachs-students-ai-chatgpt-interviews-amazon-anthropic)
- **ISE (UK employer body) data:** about half of employers worry that graduates using AI in selection misrepresent their abilities (48%). Employers frequently encountering cheating rose from 7% to 15%, and the most common misconduct was undisclosed AI use in interviews (61%). 49% had no problem with candidates using AI to draft CVs, cover letters and application answers. 33% are redesigning selection because of generative AI (up from 23% in 2024) and a further 46% are reviewing processes — [FE News](https://www.fenews.co.uk/skills/half-of-employers-concerned-student-use-of-ai-is-misrepresenting-skills/); [ISE top 10 stats 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/) (secondary coverage of ISE reports)
- **AI-polished CVs:** recruiters flag generic resumes with errors and AI words like "robust" and "meticulous". One said: "If this person didn't bother to take the time to build a resume, why would I take the time reviewing it and interviewing them" (US, Feb 2025) — [Crain's New York](https://prod.crainsnewyork.com/banking-finance/hiring-junior-bankers-battle-against-ai-written-resumes)
- **London sameness:** CVs are "almost perfectly the same", and strong-looking candidates often score poorly at interview — [Freshminds, May 2026](https://www.freshminds.co.uk/blog/2026/05/event-review-investment-banking-and-the-application-arms-race)
- **AI cold emails:** 15 identical AI-written emails reached one recruiter — [Prospect Rock Partners](https://prospectrockpartners.com/15-students-just-sent-me-the-exact-same-cutting-your-teeth-in-finance-cold-email-lets-talk-about-ai-and-networking/)
- **Generic or exit-focused motivation:** vague answers get challenged with "why not S&T/corporate finance?". Sounding like you're planning your exit is the "WORST possible answer" — [IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/investment-banking-interview-questions); [Wall Street Mastermind](https://wallstmastermind.com/why-investment-banking-how-to-answer-this-critical-interview-question/)
- **Exaggeration and rambling:** inflating your responsibilities and over-talking are listed as common mistakes — [Ask Ivy](http://www.askivy.net/articles/investment-banking/interview-preparation/common-mistakes-in-investment-banking-interviews); [Sell Side Handbook, things not to say](http://sellsidehandbook.com/2017/09/12/things-not-say-investment-banking-interview/)
- **Application errors (UK spring weeks):** reusing a later-year CV unchanged, giving no division preference, and missing early openings (careers pages from August) — [Finbound, spring week guide](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-application-guide)
- **Firm knowledge:** a vague division preference is a red flag at Goldman, and not knowing the 14 Business Principles is a common mistake — [IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/goldman-sachs-interview)
- **Internship behaviour (older eFC, 2017):** heavy drinking or flirting at events damages reputations; office-politics missteps (networking into another team, then turning down the original team's offer); security lapses. A former Goldman partner advised spending the early weeks listening more than talking — [eFinancialCareers, 15 ways to screw up your internship](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2017/06/15-ways-to-screw-up-your-banking-internship)

### Inferences
- Rule of thumb for 2025-26: AI as research assistant and editor (allowed or tolerated by about half of UK employers) is fine. AI as author or live interview helper is a red flag or disqualifying. Everything on the CV should be something you can talk about for five minutes without notes.
- Arrogance: no sourced London banker quote was found. The listening-over-talking advice and the "collaboration not dominance" scoring in group exercises point the same way: confident and curious, never performative.

### Gaps
- No London-specific banker or recruiter quotes on arrogance or "know-it-all" behaviour. Financial News (fnlondon.com) was inaccessible.
- No data on how often London banks reject candidates for AI-written answers specifically.

## 7. Performance during spring week/internship to convert

### Takeaway
At spring week, banks assess potential, attitude and fit, not technical mastery. Conversion to a summer offer varies widely by bank (estimates roughly 20-70%) and usually hinges on engagement, smart questions, a final assessment or task, and how analysts and associates see you. At summer internships, junior bankers' feedback in the end-of-programme "roundtable" decides offers. Attention to detail, reliability, a positive attitude and having several advocates among the analysts are what top interns do differently.

### Cited Findings
- Spring-week conversion estimates from prep guides range from about 20-40% to 20-70% depending on bank and year — [M&I, spring weeks](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/); [AccessECS, spring week 2026](https://www.accessecs.com/blog/investment-banking-spring-week-2026-complete-guide); [RT Mentoring](https://rtmentoring.com/spring-weeks-london-2027-timeline.html) (exact attribution of each range among these not verified). A single commercial source claims J.P. Morgan converts about 50% of spring interns (unverified) — [Elite Careers Strategy, Medium](https://elitecareersstrategy.medium.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-enter-investment-banking-from-a-recruiter-who-placed-candidates-at-goldman-1d595ed0dd4e)
- Banks judge engagement, intelligent questions and meaningful contribution to tasks. The bar is potential, not technical mastery. Interns produce no real work product, so networking and following up with everyone you meet matter more. Bankers look for reliability, decent industry knowledge and evidence that you learned during the week. You're observed in presentations, at networking events and in casual chats with analysts — [AccessECS](https://www.accessecs.com/blog/investment-banking-spring-week-2026-complete-guide); [M&I spring weeks](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/); [IB Interview Questions, spring weeks UK](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/spring-weeks-uk-investment-banking-guide)
- Banks assess personality, cultural fit, engagement and demonstrated interest as much as technical skill. There's usually an assignment during the spring week that decides the summer offer (not always) — [eFinancialCareers, spring weeks](https://www.efinancialcareers.fi/news/spring-week-bank)
- If the spring week doesn't convert, you can still apply through the normal route for summer internships, and off-cycle roles are another path — [AccessECS](https://www.accessecs.com/blog/investment-banking-spring-week-2026-complete-guide); [M&I, London accelerated recruiting](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-accelerated-recruiting-london)
- Summer internships: analysts and associates who worked most closely with interns "have significant clout" on offers. Win at least one strong advocate, ideally several, and don't bypass juniors to court senior bankers — [M&I, internship guide](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-internship-guide/)
- Analysts compare notes on which interns are reliable, so every assignment counts. "Attention to detail above all", reliability and communication are the main criteria. A "roundtable" near the end of the internship decides offers — [IB Interview Questions, return offers](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/blog/how-to-get-return-offer-summer-internship)
- London summer internships last about 9-10 weeks and are the main feeder into graduate analyst roles — [Lancaster University](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/lums/our-departments/accounting-and-finance/blogs/investment-banking-spring-weeks); [IB Analysts, UK timeline](https://investmentbankinganalysts.com/uk-investment-banking-recruiting-timeline-year-spring-weeks-summer-full-time/)
- Former Goldman partner (2017): listen more than you talk in the early weeks. Avoid drinking-culture missteps and office politics — [eFinancialCareers 2017](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2017/06/15-ways-to-screw-up-your-banking-internship)

### Inferences
- A spring-week routine for top performers, built from the sources: before the week, research every team and speaker and prepare 2-3 sharp questions per session tied to a recent deal or market event. During the week, meet as many analysts and associates as possible, note one personal detail per person the same evening, contribute in the group task by structuring and summarising rather than dominating, and treat the final assignment like a client deliverable (error-free). After the week, send a personalised thank-you within 48 hours to each person met, and follow up later with an update (e.g. "I built a model of X after our chat").
- For summer internships: triple-check all output (numbers, formatting, spelling, sources), confirm instructions back in writing, give proactive status updates, and own mistakes immediately. Build relationships with analysts first, since they speak first at the roundtable.

### Gaps
- No official 2025-26 conversion rates published by banks. All numbers are prep-provider estimates.
- No recent (2024-26) London banker-quoted "what top interns do" material. The eFC intern-mistakes piece is from 2017.

## 8. Mindset and long game: personal brand, mentors, consistency; first-year vs second-year sequencing

### Takeaway
For UK students the long game is shaped by the calendar. First year: spring week applications (opening from about August-September, rolling, early is better) plus a commercial-awareness habit and one serious society role. Second year: summer internship applications (opening about July-September), backed by proof-of-work artefacts. In the AI era, "brand" means a consistent, verifiable trail (pitches, write-ups, society roles, references) that makes your CV credible when probed.

### Cited Findings
- Spring weeks are 3-5 day programmes around Easter. Applications for the 2027 cycle opened about September-November 2026 and typically ask for a one-page CV, grades and a short motivation answer. Applying early, to 3-5 programmes you can research properly, is advised; some firms add numerical tests or HireVue — [Lancaster University](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/lums/our-departments/accounting-and-finance/blogs/investment-banking-spring-weeks); [Finbound, spring week 2027](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-application-guide)
- The only reliable source for dates is each bank's own early-careers page. Check careers pages from August — [RT Mentoring](https://rtmentoring.com/spring-weeks-london-2027-timeline.html); [Finbound](https://www.finbound.org/blog/spring-week-application-guide)
- Eligibility: usually first-years on three-year courses. Students on four-year courses may be able to apply in second year — [IB Analysts, UK timeline](https://investmentbankinganalysts.com/uk-investment-banking-recruiting-timeline-year-spring-weeks-summer-full-time/); [Lancaster](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/lums/our-departments/accounting-and-finance/blogs/investment-banking-spring-weeks)
- Insight days are open to any degree discipline and run 1-5 days, often at Easter. Join finance societies and start learning valuation basics in first year — [Sussex Careers](https://guides.careers.sussex.ac.uk/banking/experience); [LearnSignal, UK 2026 guide](https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/how-to-become-an-investment-banker-uk/)
- A strong spring week can lead to a fast-track to the next year's summer analyst role. Full-time analyst roles are mostly offered to successful summer analysts — [IB Analysts](https://investmentbankinganalysts.com/uk-investment-banking-recruiting-timeline-year-spring-weeks-summer-full-time/); [WallStreetMojo](https://www.wallstreetmojo.com/investment-banking-spring-weeks/)
- Banks are redesigning selection to probe for authenticity (ISE: 33% redesigning, 46% reviewing). UBS now tests AI fluency — [FE News/ISE](https://www.fenews.co.uk/skills/half-of-employers-concerned-student-use-of-ai-is-misrepresenting-skills/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/at-ubs-ai-skills-are-now-a-condition-for-landing-a-job/)

### Inferences
- **First year, summer before term to December:** set up the daily commercial-awareness routine (FT front section, Companies & Markets, Lex, plus one 150-word note a day). Apply to spring weeks early (September-November). Join the finance or investment society and the student fund. Attend bank campus presentations and insight evenings, follow up with everyone, and keep a simple spreadsheet (name, bank, team, date, what you discussed, follow-up date).
- **First year, January-Easter:** spring-week interviews and HireVue (story bank ready). Write a first stock pitch. Easter: spring week(s) and insight days.
- **First year, summer:** any relevant experience (boutique, Big 4, corporate finance at a mid-sized company, a start-up finance role). Build one full model of a UK-listed company. Start a weekly deal newsletter if writing suits you. Prepare for summer-internship applications opening July-September.
- **Second year, autumn:** apply early for summer internships (fast-track if converted). Take a leadership role in a society or fund. Deepen one sector or product interest. Practise technicals and the deal discussion with peers or careers services.
- **Second year, spring and summer:** internship interviews, then the internship itself (section 7).
- Mindset: consistency beats intensity. Ten minutes a day of markets reading over 18 months beats cramming. Find two or three mentors (an alumnus analyst, a society senior who has converted, a careers adviser) and give each a short update every term. Be visibly genuine. In a world of AI-polished applicants, being specific, humble and curious is itself a differentiator.

### Gaps
- No sourced, banker-endorsed guidance on "personal branding" for UK undergraduates (e.g. LinkedIn posting) and no evidence it affects outcomes.
- No sourced evidence on structured mentoring outcomes in UK IB recruiting, apart from the existence of access programmes such as [SEO London](https://www.seo-london.org/ibandam).
- University careers pages (LSE, Oxford, Cambridge, Warwick) could not be fetched. Only Lancaster and Sussex appeared in results.
